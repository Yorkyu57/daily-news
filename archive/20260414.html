The state-owned Great British Energy – Nuclear (GBE-N) subsequently acquired the site from Hitachi in 2024. On Monday, the government announced that Rolls-Royce SMR and GBE-N had signed a contract allowing immediate commencement of work. Chancellor Rachel Reeves stated the investment would "strengthen our energy security, create skilled jobs and help to build a new generation of homegrown nuclear technology that will power our economy for decades to come." The Labour government views nuclear energy as a vital source of carbon-free electricity and a potential major export industry. Rolls-Royce, the FTSE 100 jet engine manufacturer, was selected as the preferred developer last year. The company holds a majority stake in Rolls-Royce SMR, alongside Qatar's sovereign wealth fund, France's BNF Resources, and the Czech utility CEZ. The subsidiary currently employs over 1,000 people developing technology destined for installation at Temelín in the Czech Republic. Erginbilgiç noted the deal provides "crucial contractual certainty in our domestic market that will unlock the opportunity to deploy a global fleet of Rolls-Royce SMRs," positioning the UK for a "golden age of new nuclear." Tom Greatrex, chief executive of the Nuclear Industry Association, called the agreement a "historic step for clean power, industrial growth and skilled jobs in Wales."
