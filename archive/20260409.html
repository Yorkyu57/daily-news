    [Original] Shionogi nabs initial $119M award from BARDA to establish US antibiotic plant
    On April 8, 2026, Japan-based Shionogi announced a significant new contract with the U.S. Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority (BARDA) to bolster domestic manufacturing of its antibiotic Fetroja (cefiderocol). This agreement marks Shionogi's entry into a wave of pharmaceutical companies committing to U.S. production, though under distinct circumstances focused on national security. The Osaka-headquartered firm aims to expand Fetroja's utility against "high biothreat pathogens," specifically targeting plague and melioidosis, while securing the drug as a "critical countermeasure" for difficult-to-treat infections. Fetroja originally received FDA approval in 2019 for complicated urinary tract infections, followed by a 2020 authorization for hospital-acquired bacterial pneumonia (HABP) and ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia (VABP). Under the new deal, BARDA, an agency within the Department of Health and Human Services' Administration for Strategic Preparedness and Response, has committed an initial $119 million. The contract includes multiyear options that could increase total funding to $482 million if fully exercised. Shionogi intends to utilize these funds to establish a dedicated U.S. manufacturing site for Fetroja and support its procurement, though specific details regarding capacity or location remain undisclosed. Simultaneously, the company will advance development to treat infections caused by high-priority biothreat pathogens, including *Burkholderia pseudomallei* (melioidosis) and *Yersinia pestis* (plague). Shionogi also plans to seek FDA approval to expand Fetroja's use to pediatric patients with HABP and VABP. Nathan McCutcheon, president and CEO of Shionogi Inc., stated, "Shionogi is proud of our ongoing commitment to combating antimicrobial resistance, as shown by the continued investment in Fetroja since its introduction in 2020, expanding our portfolio with the acquisition of Qpex Biopharma, Inc. in 2023, and further investing in Qpex to establish a new research facility dedicated to advancing antimicrobial research and development in 2025." He added that the contract "complements our existing work with the U.S. government and enables us to advance our ongoing expansion efforts in the U.S. at greater pace and scale." Shionogi highlighted its history of bringing six novel antibiotics to market over 70 years. The Fetroja agreement falls under BARDA's Project BioShield, designed to accelerate the R&D and availability of medical countermeasures against chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear agents. This deal aligns with BARDA's broader strategy of securing prophylactic supplies, including recent pacts for smallpox, mpox, and bird flu vaccines with partners like Bavarian Nordic, GSK, Sanofi, and CSL. Additionally, in October, Cidara Therapeutics secured a BARDA award worth up to $339 million for its experimental flu preventive, CD388, before being acquired by Merck for $9.2 billion.
