  • 推出真实世界语音均衡器：衡量语音人工智能的人类质量
    Hume AI 推出了 Real World VoiceEQ，这是一个旨在衡量语音 AI 交互中人类质量的新基准。该举措于 2026 年 7 月 15 日发布，解决了当前评估方法中的一个关键空白。虽然现有基准表明，在词错误率和延迟方面，语音 AI 正接近人类水平，但现实世界的对话揭示了显著的不足。语音模型往往无法保持说话人身份的一致性，忽略犹豫或不确定性，并在处理口音、噪音和情感化语音时遇到困难——这些细微差别是传统指标所忽视的。 Real World VoiceEQ 在 15 个关键维度和 60 多个指标上评估了 40 多个领先的专有和开源语音模型。这些评估涵盖自动语音识别（ASR）、文本转语音（TTS）、语音到语音（S2S）以及语音理解。该基准基于超过 100 万条个人人类评分，这些评分收集自多样化的人口统计特征、说话风格和声学环境。具体而言，它包括 78.5 万条 TTS 评分和 4.8 万条语音到语音（STS）评分，使其成为迄今为止最大规模的人类语音 AI 评估之一。所有评估均使用 Hume 的原生语音评估平台 Kairos 进行，该平台还允许企业运行自定义评估，并通过强化学习改进模型。 关键发现表明，语音人工智能的进步正变得越来越专业化，没有任何单一模型在所有能力组中均位列前五。领先的系统针对不同的优势进行优化，例如技术准确性、情感理解或鲁棒性。例如，一个在复述复杂药品名称方面表现出色的模型，可能在情感表达上存在不足。此外，语音模型在说话方面的表现优于倾听。语音到语音（Speech-to-Speech）模型表现出最大的差异，许多系统仍然主要依赖文本转录，而忽略了语调、节奏和犹豫等副语言线索。这种对语境解读的缺失意味着模型经常错过关键的区别，例如在银行场景中，自信地说“是”与犹豫地说“……是……”之间的差异。 该研究还强调，传统基准测试越来越倾向于高估实际表现。研究发现，在背景噪音下的语音转录词错误率大约是背景音乐下的四倍，揭示了隐藏的失败模式。此外，尽管大型语言模型（LLMs）被广泛用于评估，但研究表明应谨慎使用语音语言模型（SLMs）。在发音等可验证任务上，SLMs与人类评分者之间的一致性很高，但在涉及情感或身份一致性的主观评估中，这种一致性显著下降。Hume AI认为，人类评估对于评估声学语境和社会解读仍然至关重要，并敦促行业采用以人类为基础的标准，如现实世界VoiceEQ，以确保语音人工智能在现实世界对话的复杂性中取得成功。
    [Original] Introducing Real World VoiceEQ: Measuring the human quality of voice AI
    Hume AI has introduced Real World VoiceEQ, a new benchmark designed to measure the human quality of voice AI interactions. Published on July 15, 2026, the initiative addresses a critical gap in current evaluation methods. While existing benchmarks indicate that voice AI is nearing human-level performance in terms of word error rates and latency, real-world conversations reveal significant shortcomings. Voice models often fail to maintain consistent speaker identity, miss hesitation or uncertainty, and struggle with accents, noise, and emotional speech—nuances that traditional metrics overlook. Real World VoiceEQ evaluates more than 40 leading proprietary and open-source voice models across 15 key dimensions and over 60 metrics. These assessments cover Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), Text-to-Speech (TTS), Speech-to-Speech (S2S), and Speech Understanding. The benchmark is grounded in more than 1 million individual human ratings collected across diverse demographics, speaking styles, and acoustic environments. Specifically, it includes 785,000 TTS ratings and 48,000 Speech-to-Speech (STS) ratings, making it one of the largest human evaluations of voice AI to date. All evaluations were conducted using Kairos, Hume’s voice-native evaluation platform, which also allows enterprises to run custom evaluations and improve models through reinforcement learning. Key findings indicate that progress in voice AI is becoming increasingly specialized, with no single model ranking in the top five across all capability groups. Leading systems optimize for different strengths, such as technical accuracy, emotional understanding, or robustness. For instance, a model excelling at reciting complex pharmaceutical names may struggle with emotional expressiveness. Furthermore, voice models have become better at speaking than listening. Speech-to-Speech models showed the widest variation, with many systems remaining largely transcript-driven and ignoring paralinguistic cues like tone, pacing, and hesitation. This failure to interpret context means models often miss critical distinctions, such as the difference between a confident "Yes" and a hesitant "…yes…" in a banking scenario. The study also highlights that traditional benchmarks increasingly overestimate real-world performance. Transcription word error rates on noise-backed speech were found to be roughly four times higher than on music-backed speech, revealing hidden failure modes. Additionally, while Large Language Models (LLMs) are widely used for evaluation, the research suggests Speech-Language Models (SLMs) should be used with caution. Agreement between SLMs and human raters was high for verifiable tasks like pronunciation but declined significantly for subjective evaluations involving emotion or identity consistency. Hume AI argues that human evaluation remains essential for assessing acoustic context and social interpretation, urging the industry to adopt human-grounded metrics like Real World VoiceEQ to ensure voice AI succeeds in the complexity of real-world conversation.

The Rastafari Society of Kenya had argued that smoking cannabis is a sacred sacrament central to their faith. They sought a limited exemption allowing followers to grow, possess, and use cannabis privately during worship without fear of arrest, rather than wholesale legalization. The society maintained that this practice was an essential element of their religious doctrine that must be respected under the constitution. However, the state opposed the plea, warning that creating a religious exemption would undermine anti-drug enforcement and create loopholes for illegal trafficking. Justice Mwamuye found the evidence regarding the centrality of cannabis to the Rastafari faith to be inconsistent and insufficient. He noted that any such exemption would require a sound constitutional and legal foundation, which the petitioners did not establish. The ruling deals a significant blow to the community’s six-year legal battle. Under Kenya’s Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (Control) Act, possession for personal use carries a penalty of up to five years’ imprisonment or a fine of up to $800 (£600).
