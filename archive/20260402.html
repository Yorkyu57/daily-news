    [Original] Estate agents accuse Rightmove of charging excessive fees
    Estate agents are pursuing a class action lawsuit against the property portal Rightmove, accusing it of charging "unsustainable" and unfair fees. The claim, launched by accountant and former Competition and Markets Authority panel member Jeremy Newman on behalf of potentially hundreds of agents, seeks just under £1.5bn in damages. A letter of claim has been filed with the Competition Appeal Tribunal, alleging Rightmove has "abused a dominant position" in the market. Newman claims agents have seen fees more than double in recent years with little change in service, while being squeezed by flat property prices. "Estate agents are having to employ fewer people because they can't afford them alongside their fees to Rightmove," he said. "As a result, their services can't be as effective." He asserts the action is not about arguing Rightmove should not exist, but that it is "exploiting a self-evident dominant market position, and are charging too much for it." Rightmove, which reports a consistent profit margin of around 70% and claims an 80% share of time spent on property portals, said the claim was "without merit, and we will defend it vigorously," adding it is "confident in the value we provide." Evidence from agents reviewed by the BBC shows sharp fee rises. Alisa Zotimova, founder of AZ Real Estate in London, said her fees have "more than doubled" in seven years from a promotional rate, a rise she described as "unsustainable." She feels pushed into using the portal because customers expect it. Chris, who owns two agents in Northamptonshire, pays over £5,000 monthly for a basic membership, a cost equivalent to two full-time salaries that he says is ultimately passed to clients. "When you're trying to run and operate a small business and a particular company has complete control of that business, is a challenge," he said. Many agents acknowledge Rightmove delivers most of their leads despite being significantly more expensive than rivals like Zoopla. Andy Keogh, a Midlands agent, believes the platform gives "value for money," with 80% of his leads coming from it, and said agents complaining "would struggle with their business if they decided to come off it." His fees are rising from £1,710 to £1,850 per month. Zotimova, however, warns of market-wide consequences if smaller agents cannot compete: "For buyers, sellers, tenants and landlords there will be higher fees, less choice of agents." On learning of the legal action, she decided to join, feeling, "It feels a bit David and Goliath, now it seems there is maybe a chance."
