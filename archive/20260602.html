    [Original] 2026 Orphan Drugs Report: A safe(r) passage amid stormy waters?
    Despite policy and pricing turmoil across the pharmaceutical industry, sales of orphan drugs continue to rise steadily, according to Evaluate’s Orphan Drugs Report, “A Safe(r) passage amid story waters?”. By 2032, orphan drugs will account for over 21% of all prescription pharmaceutical sales, up from 15% in 2022, generating over $400 billion of the projected $2 trillion global prescription drug market—roughly equivalent to the entire prescription medicines market twenty years ago. The top eight orphan drugs will each sell over $6 billion worldwide in 2032. Category leader Darzalex (daratumumab), for multiple myeloma, will reach nearly twice that, aided by a subcutaneous formulation that doubles the franchise’s effective lifespan. An orphan-friendly tweak to the Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) in 2025 also supports growth. At the company level, orphan drug revenues remain concentrated among a small group of global pharmaceutical firms, but the competitive order is shifting. Johnson & Johnson is forecast to lead in 2032 with nearly $31 billion in orphan drug sales, representing about 40% of its total pharmaceutical revenues. Focused biopharma players are reshaping the landscape: Dutch-incorporated Argenx is projected to displace Pfizer in the top ten rankings, driven by its Vyvgart franchise. The orphan drug pipeline remains healthy, with sales of today’s pipeline candidates collectively forecast to top $100 billion in 2032. The most valuable late-stage orphan programs are expected to sell over $1 billion each that year; in net present value terms, the top five exceed $7 billion. However, orphan pipeline drugs’ share of overall prescription drug sales is expected to fall from a projected 30% peak in 2027 to 22% in 2032, reflecting growing investment in blockbuster therapies for common diseases, particularly GLP-1 based treatments expanding beyond diabetes and obesity. Recent U.S. policy developments have been broadly supportive. Amendments to the Inflation Reduction Act in 2025 expanded exemptions for multi-indication orphan drugs, reducing the risk of early exposure to Medicare price negotiations. The Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher program was re-authorized in early 2026, promising faster FDA reviews for rare childhood conditions. At the same time, the regulatory environment has grown more complex: the FDA has raised evidentiary standards, especially for cell and gene therapies, reinforcing the need for robust trial design and clear regulatory strategy. China’s rise is also impacting rare diseases. The share of FDA orphan designations assigned to China-based sponsors rose from less than 5% in 2020 to 15% in 2025, potentially adding pricing pressure on Western orphan drugs through increased competition. Despite these pressures, fundamentals remain strong. More than 95% of rare diseases still lack an approved treatment, leaving substantial room for growth. Advances in data science, genomics, and AI-enabled drug discovery are improving diagnosis, patient identification, and trial design, helping reduce development risk and inform better decision-making.
