    [Original] Nonprofit Current AI is racing to build the World Wide Web of AI, free for all
    Nonprofit Current AI is developing open, public AI infrastructure to create a "World Wide Web of AI" that is free and accessible to all. Founded in February 2025 by Martin Tisne, the organization aims to solve the exclusion of non-English speakers and marginalized communities from major AI systems. CEO Ayah Bdeir, who joined in January after leading Mozilla’s AI strategy and founding littleBits, emphasizes that current AI models, dominated by private companies like OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic, fail to represent hundreds of languages and dialects. In February, Current AI partnered with Bhashini, the Indian government’s AI language division, at the India AI Summit to launch Suno Sutra. This offline, pocket-sized device runs AI in 22 Indian languages without internet access, addressing the needs of rural users who may not speak English. The device is open-sourced for developer communities. Bdeir argues that because language carries culture, identity, and tradition, technology that cannot speak a user’s language cannot hold their culture. She distinguishes Current AI’s approach from Big Tech’s multilingual efforts, which she claims expand markets regardless of consent, often using Indigenous data like missionary Bible translations without community rules. Current AI operates as a public-private partnership funded by governments, companies, and philanthropies. The French government seeded the nonprofit with $100 million, joined by the Ford Foundation, MacArthur Foundation, DeepMind, and Salesforce, bringing total committed funding to $400 million. Bdeir clarifies these entities are funders, not investors. Last month, Current AI allocated $3.2 million in grants to four organizations across Kenya, Lebanon, and the Brazilian Amazon. Projects include Masakhane in Kenya, building datasets for over 50 African languages; Lebanon’s Institute for Worldmaking, digitizing Arab cultural history; Brazil’s Portal sem Porteiras, creating offline tools for Indigenous Amazon communities; and Kenya’s African Internet Rights Alliance, developing audit tools for AI accountability. The nonprofit prioritizes local data storage and community consent protocols, ensuring communities can halt processes. In Geneva, Current AI launched Alpha Chat, an open-source chatbot assembled in seven weeks by a coalition including Hugging Face, Mozilla, and MIT Media Lab. Additionally, Current AI partnered with Tokyo-based Sakana AI to build a shared open-source stack supporting Japanese culture and Global South communities. Bdeir asserts that scale is not the only measure of success, citing an Indigenous elder in the Amazon using a Kenyan-built tool to preserve ecological knowledge in their own language as a key goal.
