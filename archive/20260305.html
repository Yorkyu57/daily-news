    [Original] TikTok won't protect DMs with controversial privacy tech, saying it would put users at risk
    TikTok has confirmed it will not implement end-to-end encryption (E2EE) for direct messages, a controversial privacy feature adopted by nearly all its major rivals. While platforms like Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and X have embraced E2EE to maximize user privacy, TikTok argues the technology compromises user safety. E2EE ensures only the sender and recipient can view message contents, preventing tech firms and law enforcement from accessing material. Critics contend this hinders the ability to stop harmful content, a concern TikTok explicitly cites as its primary reason for rejecting the feature. The company told the BBC that its decision is a deliberate strategy to distinguish itself from competitors, prioritizing the protection of users, particularly young people, from harm. TikTok stated that E2EE would prevent police and safety teams from reading direct messages when necessary. Although the platform claims 30 million monthly users in the UK and over a billion globally, it faces ongoing scrutiny regarding data protection practices. Owned by Chinese tech giant ByteDance and headquartered in Los Angeles and Singapore, TikTok has long faced accusations of ties to the Chinese state. Earlier this year, US lawmakers ordered the separation of its US operations from its global business, though TikTok consistently denies risks to user data. Industry analyst Matt Navarra described the move as "savvy" but noted it carries "pretty combustible optics." He observed that TikTok can now credibly argue it prioritizes "proactive safety" over "privacy absolutism," yet the decision places the firm "out of step with global privacy expectations." While privacy experts hail E2EE as the best defense against hackers and repressive authorities, governments and child protection charities warn it enables criminals to share illegal content undetected. TikTok insists all direct messages remain secured via standard encryption, similar to Gmail, with access granted only to authorized employees in response to valid law enforcement requests or user reports. The UK's NSPCC welcomed the decision, with associate head of policy Rani Govender stating, "We know just how risky end-to-end-encrypted platforms can be for children, preventing the detection of child sexual abuse and exploitation." Similarly, Dan Sexton, chief technology officer at the Internet Watch Foundation, called the choice an "important precedent" against platforms rushing to implement encryption regardless of implications. However, cyber security professor Alan Woodward suggested "Chinese influence might be behind the decision," noting E2EE is "largely banned in China." TikTok has attempted to allay concerns through initiatives like Project Clover, which offers extra data protection layers for European customers. Observers suggest the decision may also aim to maintain support from lawmakers regarding the safeguarding of its young user base.
