  • NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Embed 在 RTEB 综合排名中位列第一，推动智能体检索技术发展
    NVIDIA 发布了 Nemotron 3 Embed，这是一系列开放且可供商业使用的嵌入模型，旨在提升生产规模检索增强生成（RAG）、智能体检索、代码检索以及智能体记忆的检索质量。此次发布解决了多步智能体工作流中的关键挑战，在这些工作流中，检索质量低下会导致获取不相关的上下文、浪费令牌预算以及在推理步骤中引入噪声。 该系列包含三个开放模型，由 Nemotron-3-Embed-8B-BF16 领衔，该模型在 RTEB 排行榜上综合排名第一。这款旗舰模型确立了该系列的质量上限，而高效的 1B 变体则针对低成本、高吞吐量的部署进行了优化。在 RTEB、ViDoRe V3 Text、MMTEB Retrieval 和 LongEmbed 上的评估显示，与上一代基线相比，使用平均 NDCG@10 衡量的检索准确率取得了显著提升。 在智能体应用场景中，更优质的检索能够通过更早返回相关证据来降低下游成本，从而减少重复搜索和不必要的推理轮次。NVIDIA 使用由 Nemotron 3 Ultra 驱动的智能体进行搜索测试，发现 8B 模型在 ViDoRe V3、BRIGHT 和 BrowseComp-Plus 基准测试中，同时实现了最高的平均检索准确率和最低的预估下游令牌成本。令牌成本是根据 GPT-5.5 定价公式估算的。 针对高吞吐量部署，Nemotron-3-Embed-1B-NVFP4 模型利用 NVIDIA Blackwell 架构的原生 NVFP4 加速功能。该模型将权重和激活值量化为 NVFP4，并采用量化感知蒸馏（QAD）以维持长输入序列的准确性。为支持企业级规模，NVIDIA 发布了针对 1B 模型优化的基于 Rust 的 NIM 微服务，其在 NVIDIA GB200 和 RTX PRO 6000 GPU 上的表现与 vLLM 检查点持平或更优。 8B 模型将 Ministral-3-8B-Instruct-2512 骨干网络适配为双向编码器，通过在源自网络和合成的文本上进行对比预训练，随后在法律、金融、医疗、商业和教育领域的多语言数据集上进行微调。1B 模型源自一个 3B 基础模型，通过采用 NVIDIA ModelOpt 的神经架构搜索进行结构化剪枝，并经过两轮来自 8B 教师模型的蒸馏而获得。 Nemotron 3 Embed 以开放权重和源训练配方发布。开发者可通过 Hugging Face、NVIDIA AI Cloud 或 NIM 微服务访问模型。在 NV Docs 上对 1B 模型进行微调后，NDCG@10 从 56.7% 提升至 63.3%，Recall@5 从 56.1% 提升至 62.8%。企业合作伙伴正在评估这些模型在智能体检索和记忆应用中的表现。
    [Original] NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Embed Ranks #1 Overall on RTEB, Advancing Agentic Retrieval
    NVIDIA has released Nemotron 3 Embed, a collection of open and commercially available embedding models designed to enhance retrieval quality for production-scale Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), agentic retrieval, code retrieval, and agent memory. The release addresses critical challenges in multi-step agentic workflows, where poor retrieval leads to irrelevant context fetching, wasted token budgets, and noise in reasoning steps. The collection features three open models, led by the Nemotron-3-Embed-8B-BF16, which ranks #1 overall on the RTEB leaderboard. This flagship model establishes the quality ceiling for the collection, while efficient 1B variants are optimized for lower-cost, high-throughput deployment. Evaluations across RTEB, ViDoRe V3 Text, MMTEB Retrieval, and LongEmbed demonstrate strong gains in retrieval accuracy using average NDCG@10 compared to prior-generation baselines. In agentic settings, better retrieval reduces downstream costs by returning relevant evidence earlier, minimizing repeated searches and unnecessary reasoning turns. Using a search agent powered by Nemotron 3 Ultra, NVIDIA found that the 8B model delivered both the highest average retrieval accuracy and the lowest estimated downstream token cost across ViDoRe V3, BRIGHT, and BrowseComp-Plus benchmarks. Token costs were estimated using the GPT-5.5 pricing formula. For high-throughput deployments, the Nemotron-3-Embed-1B-NVFP4 model utilizes native NVFP4 acceleration on NVIDIA Blackwell architectures. It quantizes weights and activations to NVFP4 and employs Quantization-Aware Distillation (QAD) to maintain accuracy for long input sequences. To support enterprise scale, NVIDIA released an optimized Rust-based NIM microservice for the 1B model, which matches or outperforms the vLLM checkpoint on NVIDIA GB200 and RTX PRO 6000 GPUs. The 8B model adapts the Ministral-3-8B-Instruct-2512 backbone into a bidirectional encoder, trained via contrastive pre-training on web-sourced and synthetic text, then fine-tuned on multilingual datasets in legal, finance, medical, business, and education domains. The 1B model was derived from a 3B base through structured pruning using NVIDIA ModelOpt’s Neural Architecture Search and two rounds of distillation from the 8B teacher. Nemotron 3 Embed is released with open weights and source training recipes. Developers can access models via Hugging Face, NVIDIA AI Cloud, or NIM microservices. Fine-tuning the 1B model on NV Docs improved NDCG@10 from 56.7% to 63.3% and Recall@5 from 56.1% to 62.8%. Enterprise partners are already evaluating the models for agentic retrieval and memory applications.

    [Original] Why AMI Labs’ Alexandre LeBrun won’t call his AI ‘AGI’ or ‘superintelligence’
    Alexandre LeBrun, CEO of AMI Labs, the world model startup founded by Yann LeCun, explicitly rejects industry buzzwords like “AGI” and “superintelligence.” In an interview with TechCrunch, LeBrun stated, “We never used the word AGI... Next time well switch to something else,” arguing that “superintelligence” lacks a useful definition. This stance comes as AMI Labs, currently pre-product, seeks industrial partners in robotics, manufacturing, and electronics to validate its technology outside the lab. LeBrun emphasizes that while large language models (LLMs) predict text, world models predict the next state of the physical world. He notes that current robots are “completely static” and “really dumb in the physical world,” lacking context awareness. He illustrated this with an example of a robot dancing at a public event potentially kicking a child due to an inability to understand surroundings. LeBrun asserts that LLMs and world models are complementary; LLMs remain efficient for language processing, while world models provide necessary real-world understanding. AMI Labs is actively scouting partners in Asia, particularly South Korea, where LeBrun recently attended the International Conference on Machine Learning. He cites Korea’s advanced industries in robotics, semiconductors, and manufacturing as key attractions, noting these hardware-heavy sectors were largely untouched by the first wave of AI. Additionally, LeBrun points to Korea’s speed as an early adopter, referencing its rapid internet adoption 25 years ago and its current national plan to invest heavily in AI. JP Lee, CEO of SBVA and an AMI backer, supports this focus, highlighting Seoul’s June plan to mobilize approximately $880 billion for chips, AI data centers, and physical AI. Lee argues that Korea’s value lies not just in hardware but in developers who quickly adopt new tools, similar to how local firms like Naver and Kakao emerged. Despite significant financial backing, AMI Labs has no product or timeline to commit to. The startup, co-founded by Turing Award winner Yann LeCun after he left Meta, raised $1.03 billion in March at a $3.5 billion pre-money valuation. LeBrun declined to provide a release schedule, stating, “Well make a surprise when were ready.” He maintains that world models cannot be built inside a lab and require access to real-world environments, reinforcing the need for close partnerships with manufacturers and industrial players to train on reality.
