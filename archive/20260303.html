  • 为何在细胞治疗生产中关闭开放步骤至关重要
    细胞治疗制造在污染、变异性和操作负担方面面临独特的脆弱性，因为活细胞无法进行最终灭菌或无菌过滤。因此，开放式操作增加了无菌风险并导致设施复杂性增加。包括欧盟 GMP 附录 1（2022 年版）和 FDA 无菌加工指南在内的监管指导要求实施全面的污染控制策略（CCS），并基于风险采用封闭式、无菌和自动化系统，以提高无菌保证水平、可扩展性和成本效益。 “开放式”步骤会将产品或接触表面暴露于房间环境中，例如未加盖的容器或在生物安全柜（BSC）中进行移液操作。这些操作依赖于 B 级背景下的 A 级单向气流，从而产生对操作人员技术和环境质量的依赖。风险包括升高的空气生物负荷、通过环境监测和无菌工艺模拟（APS）审查的依赖操作人员的变异性、高昂的设施负担以及可扩展性限制。相反，“封闭式”系统通过使用经验证的转移、一次性流路和自动化来维持密封的无菌边界，从而消除人工接触点和批次间交叉污染风险。 由于细胞疗法缺乏最终灭菌，监管机构要求最大限度地减少环境暴露，并对任何剩余的开放步骤提供明确的理由。密闭系统提供了房间分类的灵活性，允许经过验证的步骤在较低等级环境中运行，并支持在舞厅式布局中实现多产品适用性。这对于自体疗法至关重要，因为受污染的批次无法挽回，且每个批次仅服务于一名患者。密闭系统能够在不增加 B 级洁净室数量的情况下实现并行化，降低对操作人员的依赖并提高重现性。对于需要高产量的异体平台，密闭生物反应器及下游工艺对于一致地处理种子培养、扩增、收获以及灌装/完成操作是必要的。 虽然密闭系统的初期成本可能较高，但通过设施降级、减少环境监测以及减少偏差，总体费用得以降低。其优势包括更低的能耗、更高的人力效率、更高的批次成功率以及更短的周期时间。避免后期补救至关重要，因为在早期保留开放步骤会招致昂贵的可比性研究方案和审批延误。此外，密闭的模块化流程有助于更顺畅的技术转移和更高的商业产出。 为实施密闭操作，开发人员必须将流程从细胞接收映射至最终容器密闭。相关策略包括自动干浴解冻、用于激活和扩增的密闭一次性流路、密闭取样口以及预灭菌灌装歧管。通过供应商文件、泄漏测试和焊接验证来验证密闭完整性至关重要。设施设计必须围绕密闭操作进行风险评估，且 APS/培养基灌装必须反映剩余的开放操作。采用与阶段相适应的 CMC 策略符合 FDA 关于生命周期管理的指导原则。最终，减少开放步骤是确保患者安全、符合监管要求、实现可扩展性以及缩短自体及异体疗法上市时间的根本所在。
    [Original] Why it’s critical to close open steps in cell therapy manufacturing
    Cell therapy manufacturing faces unique vulnerabilities regarding contamination, variability, and operational burden because living cells cannot undergo terminal sterilization or sterile filtration. Consequently, open manipulations elevate sterility risks and drive facility complexity. Regulatory guidance, including EU GMP Annex 1 (2022) and FDA aseptic processing guidance, mandates a holistic Contamination Control Strategy (CCS) and risk-based adoption of closed, sterile, and automated systems to enhance sterility assurance, scalability, and cost efficiency. An "open" step exposes product or contact surfaces to the room environment, such as uncapped vessels or pipetting in a Biosafety Cabinet (BSC). These operations rely on Grade A unidirectional airflow within Grade B backgrounds, creating dependence on operator technique and environmental quality. Risks include elevated airborne bioburden, operator-dependent variability scrutinized via environmental monitoring and aseptic process simulation (APS), high facility burdens, and scalability limitations. Conversely, a "closed" system maintains a sealed sterile boundary using validated transfers, single-use flow paths, and automation to eliminate manual touchpoints and cross-batch risks. Since cell therapies lack terminal sterilization, regulators expect minimized environmental exposure and clear justification for any remaining open steps. Closure offers room classification flexibility, allowing validated steps to run in lower grades, and supports multi-product suitability in ballroom layouts. This is critical for autologous therapies, where contaminated runs are irretrievable and each batch serves a single patient. Closed systems enable parallelization without multiplying Grade B suites, reducing operator dependency and improving reproducibility. For allogeneic platforms requiring high volumes, closed bioreactors and downstream processes are necessary to handle seed trains, expansion, harvest, and fill/finish operations consistently. While closed systems may have higher upfront costs, overall expenses decrease through facility downgrades, reduced environmental monitoring, and fewer deviations. Benefits include lower energy footprints, improved labor efficiency, higher batch success rates, and faster cycle times. Avoiding late-stage remediation is vital, as leaving open steps early invites costly comparability packages and approval delays. Furthermore, closed, modular flows facilitate smoother technology transfer and higher commercial throughput. To implement closure, developers must map processes from cell receipt to final container closure. Strategies include automated dry bath thawing, closed single-use flow paths for activation and expansion, closed sampling ports, and pre-sterilized filling manifolds. Validating closure integrity through supplier documentation, leak testing, and weld validation is essential. Facilities must be designed around closure using risk assessments, and APS/media fills must reflect remaining open manipulations. Adopting a phase-appropriate CMC strategy aligns with FDA guidance for lifecycle management. Ultimately, reducing open steps is fundamental to ensuring patient safety, regulatory compliance, scalability, and reduced time-to-market for both autologous and allogeneic therapies.
