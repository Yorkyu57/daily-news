    [Original] Why the rise of open source AI isn’t hurting Anthropic … yet
    Decagon CEO Jesse Zhang published a provocative theory on Monday titled “Everyone is wrong about open source AI in the enterprise,” addressing a key contradiction in the AI economy: more mature AI deployments are switching to lighter, open-source models, yet overall spending on expensive state-of-the-art models has barely budged. Zhang argues that frontier and open-source models are not competitors but two phases of a life cycle. Expensive frontier models prove out new use cases, which are then passed to cheaper open-source alternatives as they mature. As mature use cases migrate, new ones arise, keeping frontier model spending stable. Zhang provides limited data, but supporting evidence is available. Vercel’s AI gateway dashboard shows DeepSeek now processes over a third of tokens on the platform, and Z.ai’s GLM-5.2 model jumped to fourth place in token volume. However, when measuring total token spend, Anthropic still accounts for more than half of all AI spending on Vercel, with its share dropping only slightly due to rising prices. OpenRouter data tells a similar story: DeepSeek V4 Flash leads in usage with 5.3 trillion weekly tokens, while the most popular frontier model, Opus 4.8, handles just over 2 trillion. Opus 4.8’s average token cost is roughly 23 times higher than V4 Flash ($1.37 per million tokens versus 6 cents), meaning Opus likely captures the majority of spending. These figures do not yet include Nvidia’s Nemotron, which is poised to lead due to Nvidia’s connections and the model’s adaptability. While the data does not fully prove Zhang’s life-cycle theory, it shows frontier labs like Anthropic are not yet suffering from open source’s rise. One explanation is that the market for AI-addressable tasks is growing so fast that top models maintain their position by dominating early-stage deployments. As Zhang puts it, “The frontier labs will keep owning discovery. Open source will increasingly own production.” Another explanation is that many use cases remain too difficult to be fully replaced by cheaper alternatives. This two-tiered economy may become a stable feature of the AI landscape. Previously, some predicted foundation labs would become commodity inputs, but frontier providers have held onto premium token pricing, and that trend appears unlikely to change soon.
