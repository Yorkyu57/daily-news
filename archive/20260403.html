    [Original] Poland, Romania must pay Pfizer $2.2B in fight over contested COVID vaccine doses: Belgian court
    On April 2, 2026, a Belgian court ruled in favor of Pfizer in a high-stakes legal dispute with Poland and Romania regarding unpaid COVID-19 vaccine orders. The Court of First Instance in Brussels ordered the two nations to pay a combined total of €1.9 billion ($2.2 billion) to the New York-based pharmaceutical giant. Specifically, Poland was directed to pay approximately €1.3 billion ($1.5 billion), while Romania must cover about €600 million ($691 million). Additionally, the court mandated that both countries accept the remaining vaccine deliveries. Poland's Ministry of Health announced on April 1 that the nation would not accept the ruling passively, stating its intention to use "all legal remedies available to it to reverse this ruling and defend its interests." The ministry confirmed the right to appeal, noting that further actions will follow an in-depth analysis of the judgment and consultations with legal representatives. Conversely, a Pfizer spokesperson told Fierce Pharma that the decision "reflects the importance of the contractual obligations that underpinned a successful European pandemic response, which was built on the principle of solidarity between Member States." The lawsuit, filed in 2023, alleged a "prolonged contractual breach" after the countries failed to pay for millions of doses of the Comirnaty vaccine developed by Pfizer and BioNTech. The dispute originated from a massive European Commission deal signed in May 2021 for 900 million doses, with an additional 200 million doses added in December 2021. This agreement faced scrutiny from Europe's Court of Auditors regarding negotiations and text messages between Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Pfizer CEO Albert Bourla. As global cases declined, Poland and other nations sought a "new, fairer deal" to address the surplus. Poland had previously invoked a force majeure clause in 2022, citing financial strain from the influx of Ukrainian refugees. However, the Brussels court rejected these arguments, determining that falling infection rates and the Ukraine war did not justify contract annulment. The court also found no evidence that Pfizer abused a dominant market position. Political fallout was immediate. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk blamed his predecessor, Mateusz Jakub Morawiecki, stating on X that Poland must pay for the "extreme stupidity" of ordering vaccines that were neither picked up nor paid for. Romanian Health Minister Alexandru Rogobete similarly attributed the situation to "poor decisions taken at the time by a previous government." Although the EU eventually struck a deal in 2023 to reduce purchased doses to match "evolving needs," the uncertainty impacted Pfizer's stock. At the J.P. Morgan Healthcare Conference in 2024, Bourla described the situation as a "big overhang," noting that governments had "second thoughts" on honoring signed contracts, with the EU deal being "one of the most famous of that."
