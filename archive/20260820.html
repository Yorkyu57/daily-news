    [Original] Aurobindo unit, China API maker dinged in latest round of FDA warning letters
    Aurobindo Pharma’s sterile injectables and oncology division, operating under its Eugia Pharma Specialties arm, has received a U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Warning Letter following an inspection of its Hyderabad, India, facilities in February. Although Eugia responded to the initial Form 483 on March 20, the FDA determined that the proposed remediations were inadequate. The regulator identified two primary issues: insufficient restricted access barrier systems in aseptic processing lines, which hindered proper ergonomics and physical separation from the surrounding environment, and frequent manual interventions by operators in critical clean room areas. The FDA also cited poor environmental monitoring at the site. These findings align with similar violations cited at other Eugia facilities inspected in November and January, both of which are classified as “Official Action Indicated” and in “unacceptable CGMP status.” The FDA stated that failures across multiple sites demonstrate inadequate management oversight. This latest reprimand follows a checkered regulatory record in 2024, during which Eugia received a warning letter at its Unit III plant in Hyderabad in August after failing to address concerns from a February 2024 inspection. Prior to that, the company had encountered four FDA Form 483s across various sites since late 2023. Simultaneously, the FDA issued a warning letter to Kilo Pharmaceutical Sci-Tech, a manufacturer of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) in Tianjin, China, based on records submitted around July 7. The agency uncovered deviations including the failure to properly register certain products intended for U.S. distribution. On the manufacturing front, Kilo failed to demonstrate that its processes are reproducible and meet necessary quality standards. Kilo was also criticized for failing to prepare and use master production and control records, and for dropping the ball on validation of testing methods and further stability testing. Regarding listing violations, the FDA noted that Kilo did not provide drug listing information for demecarium bromide and chlorambucil under its own labeler code, despite manufacturing and shipping these drugs into the United States. Although these drugs appear in the FDA’s database, they are listed under a different company’s labeler code. The FDA warned that failing to address these deviations could result in the agency “continuing to refuse admission of articles manufactured at Tianjin Kilo Pharmaceuticals Sci-Tech Co.”

    EarthGrid, a California-based engineering firm, has successfully tested a revolutionary tunnel-boring machine (TBR) capable of melting solid granite using super-heated plasma. During a January test in a California quarry, the cigar-shaped machine chewed through three metres of the ultra-hard rock. Troy Helming, EarthGrid’s founder and chief executive, described the ignition of the three plasma torches as "kind of like igniting a rocket." The torches generate plasma reaching 27,000C, a temperature significantly hotter than the surface of the Sun. Helming explained that the machine creates a "violent vortex" inside the tunnel to suck away debris, which can take the form of lava. "I actually got a little bit emotional watching it," Helming said, noting he had waited for this moment for 10 years. This emerging technology aims to make undergrounding critical infrastructure, such as electricity and telecommunications cables, data centres, and pipelines, quicker and more cost-effective. Engineering firms report rising demand for undergrounding, driven partly by Russia’s war with Ukraine, which has exposed the vulnerability of above-ground facilities to drone attacks. Helming has received interest from companies seeking to use the TBR for power, fibre optic cables, and pipelines transporting water, natural gas, or ammonia. The company is also eyeing projects for underground freight-distribution systems around airports and warehouses to reduce truck traffic. Although the January test resulted in the machine "over-boring" slightly to the top and left, Helming’s team plans to adjust the vortex to spin in alternate directions every five minutes to correct this. Commercial deployment is hoped for as early as next year. The trend toward undergrounding extends to data storage, with Alexander RE Taylor, a senior lecturer at the University of Exeter, identifying a "data bunker boom." One example is Trentino DataMine in Italy’s Dolomite Mountains, where servers are housed in caverns 100m underground. Chief executive Dennis Bonn stated that 90 million cubic metres of dolomite rock provide natural protection against physical intrusion, electromagnetic interference, and seismic events. Additionally, subsea internet cables are increasingly being buried up to three metres deep to prevent damage from ship anchors, a practice that has reduced faults per kilometre, according to Lane Burdette of TeleGeography. Robbie McGoran of Joseph Gallagher noted that countries bordering Russia are increasingly cautious about protecting infrastructure underground. While technologies like laser-guidance systems have improved accuracy, challenges remain, including dangerous gases and flooding. In the UK, Mark Neller of Arup noted that while the £1bn London Power Tunnels project, which built 18 miles (29km) of tunnels, was necessary for the busy urban area, undergrounding is often more expensive than above-ground alternatives. Richard Little, an infrastructure policy consultant, emphasized that while underground facilities make sense for critical assets like chip manufacturing, it is not feasible to put everything underground.

    [Original] Israel confirms soldiers fired at car in which Hind Rajab was killed and opens criminal investigation
    The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) has confirmed for the first time that its troops opened fire on a vehicle carrying five-year-old Palestinian girl Hind Rajab, who was killed in Gaza in 2024 along with six relatives. In a statement, the IDF announced it would initiate a criminal investigation into the incident, citing "alleged failures in the co-ordination of the movement of the Palestine Red Crescent ambulance." The military stated that its examination indicated troops fired at a vehicle traveling contrary to advanced notices published by the IDF Spokesperson. This admission contradicts the IDF’s initial claim that no troops were present in the area at the time of the killing, a suggestion that was quickly questioned after the military acknowledged conducting raids on terror targets in Gaza City neighborhoods, including Tel al-Hawa, where Hind was located. Hind’s death drew global condemnation after a recording of her desperate phone call to paramedics was broadcast. Operator Rana Faqih remained on the line for hours as Hind pleaded for rescue, describing tanks moving toward her car. Faqih told the BBC, "At one point, she told me it was getting dark... She was scared. She asked me how far away my house was. I felt paralysed and helpless." Three hours after the call began, an ambulance crewed by Yusuf al-Zeino and Ahmed al-Madhoun was cleared to approach the location. The crew notified operators they were nearing the site and awaiting entry checks by Israeli forces. It was the last communication received from the paramedics or Hind. The Palestinian Red Crescent Society confirmed both medics were killed when the ambulance was bombed, stating the Israeli occupation "deliberately targeted" the crew despite prior coordination. The story was later adapted into the Oscar-nominated film *The Voice of Hind Rajab*, and the UN cited the case in a commission of inquiry accusing Israel of war crimes, an accusation Israel denies. The IDF also announced a criminal investigation into the killing of 15 Palestinians, including medics and a UN worker, in March 2025. An IDF unit fired on ambulances, a fire engine, and a marked UN vehicle in the Tal al-Sultan area of Rafah. Initially, the military claimed troops fired on "suspicious vehicles" with lights off, but later retracted this account after a video from the phone of killed paramedic Rifaat Radwan showed the convoy using emergency lights. The bodies were found in shallow graves next to the crushed vehicles a week later. In three other cases, including the killing of seven World Central Kitchen employees in Deir al-Balah and four Médecins Sans Frontières staff, the IDF stated it found no grounds for criminal investigations. The military emphasized that for over a thousand days, it has conducted intensive combat across numerous fronts and examines exceptional incidents under international and domestic law. The investigations aim to counter accusations of impunity and stave off international legal action, notably from the International Criminal Court. However, Ziv Stahl, executive director of the Israeli human rights group Yesh Din, noted that "almost no indictments are filed" in relation to complaints against soldiers, and convictions, when they occur, result in "disproportionately lenient sentences."

His firing sparked nationwide protests, leading to Syrskyi’s replacement within days. However, in his latest address, he shifted focus to broader political reform, calling for "a state where institutions are stronger than names." Zelensky’s office has not directly responded to the video, though a source described the call for elections as "highly bizarre" and a "complete lack of realism" given the ongoing missile and drone attacks. In a related development, Ukraine’s anti-corruption agencies launched a special operation targeting current and former MPs and an unnamed presidential official.
