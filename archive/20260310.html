    [Original] Sotyktu take 2: BMS' first-in-class pill gains FDA nod to treat psoriatic arthritis
    The FDA has approved Bristol Myers Squibb’s Sotyktu for adults with active psoriatic arthritis, expanding the drug's use beyond its original 2022 approval for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis. This makes Sotyktu the first selective allosteric tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) inhibitor approved for this indication. The approval is a significant milestone for BMS, which acquired the drug through its $74 billion purchase of Celgene in 2019. The regulatory decision is supported by data from two trials. In these studies, 54% of patients on a daily 6 mg dose of Sotyktu achieved a 20% improvement in disease activity at Week 16, compared with 39% and 34% on placebo in the respective trials. For a higher bar of 50% symptom improvement, 24% and 29% of Sotyktu patients met the mark versus 14% and 16% on placebo. Al Reba, BMS’s commercial lead in cardiovascular and immunology, stated the approval “confirms its important role in managing both skin and joint symptoms of psoriatic disease.” Dr. Philip Mease of Providence Swedish Medical Center emphasized the need for new oral treatments, noting the condition often impairs movement and causes pain in joints, tendons, or ligaments. The approval arrives as Sotyktu faces impending competition. Takeda is preparing regulatory filings this year for its TYK2 inhibitor, zasocitinib, which it acquired for $4 billion. Johnson & Johnson is also developing an oral IL-23 inhibitor, icotrokinra, which has outperformed Sotyktu on secondary endpoints in psoriasis trials. For BMS, the new indication is an opportunity to boost Sotyktu's commercial performance. While two other drugs approved in BMS's 2022 wave—Opdualag and Camzyos—became blockbusters with 2025 sales of $1.2 billion and $1.1 billion respectively, Sotyktu generated $291 million last year. The company had previously made a major strategic bet on Sotyktu, choosing to retain it during the Celgene acquisition and selling the psoriasis drug Otezla to Amgen for $13.4 billion to address antitrust concerns. Otezla’s sales have since declined to $1.8 billion in 2025.
