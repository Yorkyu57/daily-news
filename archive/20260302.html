  • 为何在细胞治疗生产中关闭开放式步骤至关重要
    细胞治疗制造在污染、变异性和操作负担方面面临独特的脆弱性，因为活细胞无法进行最终灭菌或无菌过滤。因此，开放式操作会提高无菌风险并增加设施复杂性。包括欧盟 GMP 附录 1（2022 年版）和 FDA 无菌加工指南在内的监管指导要求实施全面的污染控制策略（CCS），并基于风险采用封闭式、无菌和自动化系统，以提升无菌保证水平、可扩展性和成本效益。 “开放式”步骤会将产品或接触表面暴露于房间环境中，例如未加盖的容器或在生物安全柜（BSC）中进行移液操作。这些操作通常发生在 B 级背景下的 A 级单向流环境中，高度依赖操作人员的技术。这种方法导致对空气中生物负荷的暴露增加、受环境监控和无菌工艺模拟（APS）审查的依赖操作人员的变异性、高昂的设施负担以及可扩展性受限。相反，“封闭式”系统通过使用经验证的连接器、管路焊接、一次性组件或自动化硬件来维持密封的无菌边界，从而消除产品暴露于房间环境的风险。 由于细胞疗法缺乏最终灭菌，监管机构期望最大限度地减少环境暴露，并对任何剩余的开放式步骤提供明确的理由。封闭工艺具有显著优势：它降低了污染概率，允许在较低等级房间分类下具备灵活性，并支持在舞厅式布局中适用于多种产品。对于需要并行生产患者特异性批次的自体疗法，封闭和自动化平台能够在不增加 B 级套间数量或不依赖专业操作人员的情况下实现可扩展性。对于处理大量产品的异体疗法，封闭式生物反应器及下游工艺是实现实际规模放大的关键。 尽管封闭系统的初始成本可能更高，但由于设施降级、环境监测减少以及偏差减少，总体费用通常会降低。其优势包括更小的洁净室占地面积、更低的能耗、更高的劳动力效率以及更高的批次成功率。此外，封闭系统可加快生产周期，并避免代价高昂的后期补救措施，而此类补救措施可能引发广泛的可比性研究方案和审批延误。同时，它还能通过标准化、模块化的流程促进更顺畅的技术转移。 要有效实施封闭系统，开发者必须将流程从细胞接收映射至最终容器密封。关键步骤包括采用自动干浴解冻、用于筛选和扩增的封闭一次性流路、封闭取样口以及预灭菌灌装歧管。通过供应商文件、泄漏测试和焊接验证来确认封闭完整性至关重要。设施设计应围绕封闭系统展开，并基于风险评估进行，且 APS/培养基灌装必须反映实际生产操作。采用与阶段相适应的 CMC 策略符合 FDA 的全生命周期指导原则。归根结底，减少开放步骤是确保患者安全、符合监管要求、具备可扩展性，并缩短自体及异体疗法上市时间的根本所在。
    [Original] Why it’s critical to close open steps in cell therapy manufacturing
    Cell therapy manufacturing faces unique vulnerabilities regarding contamination, variability, and operational burden because living cells cannot undergo terminal sterilization or sterile filtration. Consequently, open manipulations elevate sterility risks and drive facility complexity. Regulatory guidance, including EU GMP Annex 1 (2022) and FDA aseptic processing guidance, mandates a holistic Contamination Control Strategy (CCS) and risk-based adoption of closed, sterile, and automated systems to enhance sterility assurance, scalability, and cost efficiency. An "open" step exposes product or contact surfaces to the room environment, such as uncapped vessels or pipetting in a Biosafety Cabinet (BSC). These operations typically occur in Grade A unidirectional airflow within Grade B backgrounds, relying heavily on operator technique. This approach introduces elevated exposure to airborne bioburden, operator-dependent variability scrutinized via environmental monitoring and aseptic process simulation (APS), high facility burdens, and scalability limitations. Conversely, a "closed" system maintains a sealed sterile boundary using validated connectors, tubing welds, single-use assemblies, or automated hardware, eliminating product exposure to the room environment. Since cell therapies lack terminal sterilization, regulators expect minimized environmental exposure and clear justification for any remaining open steps. Closing processes offers significant advantages: it reduces contamination probability, allows for room classification flexibility in lower grades, and supports multi-product suitability in ballroom layouts. For autologous therapies requiring parallel patient-specific batches, closed and automated platforms enable scalability without multiplying Grade B suites or relying on specialized operators. For allogeneic therapies handling high volumes, closed bioreactors and downstream processes are essential for practical scale-up. While closed systems may have higher upfront costs, overall expenses often decrease due to facility downgrades, reduced environmental monitoring, and fewer deviations. Benefits include smaller cleanroom footprints, lower energy consumption, improved labor efficiency, and higher batch success rates. Furthermore, closure accelerates cycle times and avoids costly late-stage remediation, which can trigger extensive comparability packages and approval delays. It also facilitates smoother technology transfer through standardized, modular flows. To implement closure effectively, developers must map processes from cell receipt to final container closure. Key steps include using automated dry bath thawing, closed single-use flow paths for selection and expansion, closed sampling ports, and pre-sterilized filling manifolds. Validating closure integrity through supplier documentation, leak testing, and weld validation is critical. Facilities should be designed around closure using risk assessments, and APS/media fills must reflect actual operations. Adopting a phase-appropriate CMC strategy aligns with FDA lifecycle guidance. Ultimately, reducing open steps is fundamental to ensuring patient safety, regulatory compliance, scalability, and reduced time-to-market for both autologous and allogeneic therapies.
