    [Original] FDA citizen petition calls on agency to tighten up CRL disclosure practices
    A citizen petition filed by Covington & Burling on behalf of an unnamed pharmaceutical company is urging the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to tighten its procedures for disclosing drug rejection letters, arguing that the agency’s new “radical transparency” policy risks exposing confidential business information and misleading the public. The petition responds to a shift in FDA practice that began last summer under Commissioner Marty Makary, M.D., as part of broader transparency efforts. Previously, the agency protected the privacy of complete response letters (CRLs) sent to drugmakers, with most public releases occurring through Freedom of Information Act requests or voluntary disclosures. Now, the FDA publicly posts redacted CRLs in large batches shortly after rejections, aiming to help other developers avoid “playing a guessing game when navigating the FDA,” Makary has said. Covington argues that this practice violates federal statutes protecting confidential information and could cause “competitive harm” to drugmakers. The petition also warns that disclosing rejection letters for unapproved applications may “mislead the public” and create “unjustified concerns” about a product’s safety and efficacy if it is ultimately approved. It calls on the FDA to ensure proper redaction of all confidential commercial information and to give drugmakers an opportunity to engage with the agency on redactions before public release. “Creating a mechanism for manufacturers to provide input on redactions before posting of CRLs would protect both current and future applicants affected by this interpretational change that was made without any opportunity for notice or comment,” the attorneys wrote. The filing cites specific examples from the agency’s release of 127 public CRLs for unapproved drug applications, including rejections for Lykos Therapeutics and Stealth BioTherapeutics. According to the petition, in at least 61 of those letters, the fact that a rejection had been issued was not public at the time, and in at least 36 cases, the underlying applications had not been publicly disclosed or acknowledged. The petition comes weeks after Makary outlined a fiscal year 2027 policy wish list to Congress, in which he expressed a desire for explicit FDA authority to publicly disclose “certain information” related to safety and efficacy deficiencies in CRLs, arguing it could “make drug development more efficient for multiple stakeholders.”
