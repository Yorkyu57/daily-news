    [Original] Ionis slashes Tryngolza's price tag by 93% ahead of anticipated label expansion
    Ionis Pharmaceuticals announced a dramatic price reduction for its drug Tryngolza (olezarsen) as it prepares for a potential label expansion into severe hypertriglyceridemia (sHTG). Initially approved in 2024 for familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) with an annual list price of $595,000, the company revealed a new wholesale acquisition cost (WAC) of $40,000 annually. This updated pricing, effective April 1, will apply to both the current FCS indication and the anticipated sHTG use. Ionis stated the decision follows a "thorough review of robust provider research and extensive payer engagement," aiming to reflect "substantial value" while supporting "timely and sustained patient access." The price adjustment is strategically timed ahead of the FDA's expected decision on the sHTG application by June 30. By aligning with annual payer contracting cycles beginning in April, Ionis seeks a "proactive alignment" to ensure a "seamless launch" and secure a "first-mover advantage" in the sizable market, according to Leerink analysts. This aggressive pricing positions Tryngolza against rival Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals' Redemplo, which launched for FCS in November at a list price of $60,000 annually. Leerink analysts noted the $40,000 price point places Ionis in a "sweet spot" to contract "aggressively." Tryngolza's potential sHTG launch is underpinned by what William Blair analyst Myles Minter, Ph.D., termed "best-case scenario" data from the largest sHTG study ever conducted. Phase 3 trials demonstrated placebo-adjusted triglyceride reductions of 55% and 72% after six months, alongside an 85% lower risk of acute pancreatitis over one year. Citi analysts described the findings as a "home run." In February, the FDA granted priority review, a move CEO Brett Monia, Ph.D., called a "significant step toward our goal of delivering the first-ever treatment shown to reduce the risk of potentially life-threatening acute pancreatitis attacks in people with sHTG." Sam Tsimikas, M.D., Ionis' chief of global cardiovascular development, added that this represents an "unprecedented and historical achievement" in lipidology after nearly 50 years of effort. Targeting a U.S. population of 3 million, including 1 million high-risk sHTG patients, Ionis projects peak sales exceeding $2 billion across both indications. The company doubled its peak revenue forecast in January based on increased projected sales volumes. Tryngolza generated $108 million in 2025 sales following its 2024 FDA approval, marking Ionis' first solo product launch after years of licensing. Meanwhile, Arrowhead remains behind on the regulatory trail, having received a breakthrough therapy designation for Redemplo in sHTG last year. Arrowhead's FCS approval came a year after Tryngolza's, following a legal dispute where Ionis accused Arrowhead of copying its mRNA technology.
