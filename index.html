  • 推出 Cosmos 3 Edge
    英伟达发布了 Cosmos 3 Edge，这是一个拥有 40 亿参数的开放世界模型，旨在使物理人工智能系统能够理解、推理并在现实环境中采取行动。该模型于 2026 年 7 月 20 日发布，并可在 Hugging Face 上获取，能够在内存受限的边缘设备上提供数据中心级别的性能，包括英伟达 RTX PRO GPU、DGX 系统、GeForce RTX GPU 以及 Jetson 模块，如新发布的 Jetson T2000 和 T3000。 Cosmos 3 Edge 作为一个紧凑的视觉语言模型（VLM）和一个后训练的世界动作模型（WAM）运行。它以机器人控制分辨率（640×360 观测值）运行，在英伟达 Jetson Thor 上每次推理生成 32 个动作，同时实现 15 Hz 的实时控制。在类似的 40 亿参数模型中，它在 VANTAGE-Bench 视觉分析基准测试中排名第一，并为机器人策略学习树立了新的最先进水平。 该架构利用两个带有独立归一化层和多层感知器的 Transformer 塔，共享多模态注意力层以对齐语言、视频、音频和动作数据。这种设计使模型能够在生成输出之前对场景进行推理，对语言使用因果注意力，对扩散令牌使用更广泛的注意力。通过将各种物理动作——如车辆自身姿态、相机运动或机器人夹爪状态——映射为紧凑的几何向量，该模型在控制输入和视觉世界结构之间建立了直接联系。 在策略模式下，Cosmos 3 Edge 预测动作及其预期的视觉后果，实现双向推理，使模型能够从效果推断动作，或从动作预测效果。英伟达还发布了 Cosmos 3 Edge Policy（DROID），该模型在 DROID 数据集上针对抓取和放置任务进行了后训练，并附带训练脚本。开发者可以在 H100 集群或 DGX 工作站上对模型进行微调，然后再部署到边缘平台。 为加速采用，NVIDIA 提供了参考的预训练后检查点，包括 Cosmos 3 Super 4 步蒸馏检查点。这将扩散步骤从 35–50 步减少至仅 4 步，在保持质量的同时实现高达 25 倍的推理加速。未来的更新将专注于交互式世界生成、驾驶场景模拟以及机器人策略，并对 vLLM 等框架进行优化，以降低下游模型构建的计算需求。
    [Original] Introducing Cosmos 3 Edge
    NVIDIA has released Cosmos 3 Edge, a 4-billion-parameter open world model designed to enable physical AI systems to understand, reason about, and act within real-world environments. Published on July 20, 2026, and available on Hugging Face, the model delivers data center–level performance on memory-constrained edge devices, including NVIDIA RTX PRO GPUs, DGX systems, GeForce RTX GPUs, and Jetson modules, such as the newly announced Jetson T2000 and T3000. Cosmos 3 Edge operates as a compact vision language model (VLM) and a post-trained world action model (WAM). It functions at robot-control resolution (640×360 observations), generating 32 actions per inference on NVIDIA Jetson Thor while achieving real-time control at 15 Hz. Among similar 4B parameter models, it ranks #1 on VANTAGE-Bench for vision analytics and sets the state of the art for robot policy learning. The architecture utilizes two transformer towers with separate normalization layers and multilayer perceptrons, sharing multimodal attention layers to align language, video, audio, and action data. This design allows the model to reason about scenes before generating outputs, using causal attention for language and broader attention for diffusion tokens. By mapping diverse physical actions—such as vehicle ego pose, camera motion, or robot gripper states—into compact geometric vectors, the model creates a direct connection between control inputs and visual world structure. In policy mode, Cosmos 3 Edge predicts actions alongside their expected visual consequences, enabling bidirectional reasoning where the model can infer actions from effects or predict effects from actions. NVIDIA is also releasing Cosmos 3 Edge Policy (DROID), post-trained on the DROID dataset for pick-and-place tasks, along with training scripts. Developers can fine-tune the model on H100 clusters or DGX Stations before deploying to edge platforms. To accelerate adoption, NVIDIA provides reference post-trained checkpoints, including the Cosmos 3 Super 4-Step Distillation checkpoint. This reduces diffusion steps from 35–50 to just 4, delivering up to 25× faster inference while preserving quality. Future updates will focus on interactive world generation, driving scenario simulation, and robotics policies, with optimizations for frameworks like vLLM to reduce compute requirements for downstream model building.

    [Original] YouTube clarifies policies around AI slop and upsetting videos
    YouTube has clarified its monetization policies to further crack down on low-quality artificial intelligence content, commonly referred to as "AI slop." The updated guidelines, effective July 16, define three specific categories of "inauthentic content" that are ineligible for monetization through the YouTube Partner Program (YPP). This move aims to discourage creators from using AI tools to mass-produce repetitive or distressing videos, protecting the platform’s financial interests as it competes with traditional TV and streaming services for advertising revenue. Trust and Safety Chief Matt Halprin explained that while AI can enhance creativity, it also enables "content farming"—the rapid production of generic, low-value videos. The new policy distinguishes between high-quality AI-assisted content and low-effort material. The first prohibited category includes generic, repetitive, or template-based content, such as cookie-cutter videos made with AI or computer-generated imagery that lack narrative arcs or originality. This also covers tutorial videos that merely reproduce prevalent content rather than offering unique value. The second category targets "off-putting" or distressing content designed to manipulate emotions for views. Halprin cited examples such as videos featuring animals in distress followed by rescues, noting that viewers find such material unpleasant and are unlikely to return to those channels. Channels dedicated to this type of content will be removed from the YPP, regardless of whether AI was used in their creation. The third category restricts the use of AI personas—digital representations of real people—to discuss sensitive topics, including finance, legal issues, healthcare, and medical matters. YouTube seeks to prevent the incentivization of AI-generated figures providing advice on these critical subjects. Halprin emphasized that any channel with excessive amounts of these three content types will lose monetization privileges. The policy update adds nuance to existing rules established last year, which already barred revenue generation from mass-produced and repetitive content. By refining these definitions, YouTube aims to maintain the quality of its platform, which currently generates more ad revenue than rival streamers and has surpassed Netflix in average daily views worldwide. The clarifications apply to all YPP members, ensuring that the platform’s lifeblood remains free from low-quality fare that could undermine its competitive standing in the digital advertising market.

    Cardano activated its Van Rossem hard fork on July 18, upgrading the network to protocol version 11. This update lowers smart contract execution costs and establishes the technical and procedural foundation for the planned Ouroboros Leios scalability upgrade, expected in 2026. Crucially, this marks the first time in Cardano’s history that a hard fork was proposed, debated, and ratified entirely through its onchain governance system, rather than by founding developer Input Output. This shift signifies a transition in who controls protocol changes, moving authority from the creators to the network’s participants. The upgrade was enacted at 21:44 UTC after ratification on July 13, requiring sign-off from three distinct bodies. Delegated representatives voted 78.97% in favor, surpassing the 60% threshold. The constitutional committee, which assesses compliance with the Cardano Constitution, found the fork compliant with all seven members agreeing, exceeding the five required. Pool operators approved the upgrade by the narrowest margin, with 53.02% voting in favor, highlighting that decentralized governance does not guarantee the founders’ preferred outcomes. Additionally, network telemetry showed roughly 93% of block production was already on version 11, well above the 85% adoption requirement. Technically, version 11 is an intra-era hard fork that does not alter transaction structures, minimizing ecosystem disruption. It enhances Plutus, the platform for writing Cardano smart contracts, by unifying built-in functions across versions so older applications can access newer features. The upgrade also tightens ledger validation rules, including a provision preventing two stake pools from reusing the same cryptographic identity key. As Input Output noted in a development report, “As well as Plutus improvements and Plutus Cost Model enhancements, this upgrade lays the foundation for the next upgrade, the Dijkstra era hard fork, which will introduce Ouroboros Leios to Cardano.” For everyday users, there are no immediate visible changes; ADA transactions, wallets, and fees remain unchanged. However, the Plutus updates lower the computational costs of running smart contracts, potentially allowing developers to charge less per transaction if they rebuild their applications. The hard fork is named for Max van Rossem, a governance contributor who helped shape the network’s constitution and died in October 2025. Ultimately, Van Rossem demonstrates that Cardano holders can approve protocol changes independently, offering a formal vote in the network’s direction rather than relying on top-down development.
