Ben Bajarin, chief executive of technology consultancy Creative Strategies, said the dedicated product role signals that OpenAI is beginning to think about its products less as tools for individual productivity and more as technology designed for households. He noted this is similar to the path Google, Apple, and Meta eventually followed, but AI raises the stakes because the assistant is not just mediating content or devices. The shift brings new trust and safety challenges. Stephen Balkam, chief executive of the Family Online Safety Institute, said the hiring reflects both the maturation of OpenAI and a growing recognition that AI products used by children require different safeguards. “I see this as safety by redesign,” Balkam told TechCrunch. “You take the initial product or service that was released… not really with kids in mind… so this is a much-needed reaction and response.” His comments come as new research by the Family Online Safety Institute found that parents are underestimating how often their children use generative AI. While 27% of U.S. parents said their child had used generative AI in the past week, 38% of children reported doing so, according to a survey of more than 4,000 families in the U.S. and Australia. Balkam said AI companies should build products with stronger content controls, age-appropriate experiences, parental oversight, and reminders that users are interacting with AI. The hiring also comes amid growing scrutiny of how AI companies protect younger users. OpenAI has faced multiple lawsuits from parents alleging that ChatGPT contributed to harm suffered by their children, including in cases involving suicide. In response, OpenAI has introduced safety measures over the past year, including parental controls for teen accounts, routing sensitive conversations to reasoning models, and an optional Trusted Contact feature that can alert a family member in cases of potential self-harm. Balkam said AI companies have an opportunity to avoid the mistakes made by social media platforms, which treated children much like adults before adding stronger safeguards amid public pressure. The hiring also aligns with OpenAI’s broader efforts around families, including a recent workshop with the San Antonio Spurs Community Impact organization and the Positive Coaching Alliance to explore AI’s role in learning and youth engagement. Sensor Tower estimates that users aged 25 to 34 account for 40% of global app audiences for Anthropic’s Claude and Google’s Gemini, matching ChatGPT, compared with 33% for Microsoft’s Copilot. Copilot skews older, with 20% of its users aged 45 and above, compared with 14% for Claude, 12% for Gemini, and 11% for ChatGPT. While ChatGPT remains underpenetrated among older users, it is adding them faster than rivals. Among U.S. smartphone users who are parents, Gemini had the widest reach at 32% in Q2, followed by ChatGPT at 24%, Claude at 4%, and Copilot at 2%. For Bajarin, the hiring signals where consumer AI is headed. As AI becomes a technology shared across generations, he expects companies to roll out family plans, child and teen profiles, caregiver tools, shared household memory, AI tutoring, and stronger safety controls.
