    美国食品药品监督管理局已将飞利浦伟康特定Trilogy呼吸机的召回列为最严重类型，警告称若未修正继续使用可能导致严重伤害或死亡。此次召回涉及通过软件更新修正设备问题，无需停用或撤下销售。 飞利浦已确认影响Trilogy Evo平台呼吸机的三个问题，涉及Trilogy Evo、Evo O2、EV300及Evo Universal型号。第一个问题涉及雾化器使用风险。当在AC-VC、SIMV或AVAPS模式下使用气动或非气动雾化器与被动回路串联时，临床医生可能观察到潮气量输送与监测数值存在差异。气动雾化器可能影响所有回路类型的呼吸机性能。额外气体或气体特性变化可能影响容量输送精度，导致显示的患者容量与流量监测无法反映实际数值。触发灵敏度也可能受影响。雾化颗粒可能影响回路组件，包括外部流量传感器和被动呼气阀。潜在危害包括通气不足、患者不适及临床响应延迟。此外，内部流量传感器上的气溶胶积聚可能导致潮气量过度输送、FiO2输送不足或呼吸机无法工作状态，进而引发肺过度充气、血氧饱和度降低或治疗中断。 第二个问题涉及延迟阻塞报警。在特定通气模式下，报警可能无法在两个呼吸周期或五秒的规定时间内触发，延迟时间最长可达四次呼吸。 截至3月6日，飞利浦已报告三例与此类问题相关的严重伤害事件，无死亡报告。 飞利浦于3月2日发布了一封信函，并于4月24日发布了后续更新指南。该公司建议将所有Trilogy Evo平台呼吸机更新至软件版本1.05.15.00，并查阅最新的用户手册附录。建议用户在使用雾化器与受影响设备配合时谨慎操作。 这些呼吸机为体重至少2.5公斤的儿童至成年患者提供持续气道正压通气（CPAP）和间歇性正压通气支持，适用于医院、医疗机构及非紧急转运场景。EV300和Evo型号也可用于家庭环境。 有疑问的美国耐用医疗设备（DME）及家庭护理客户可拨打飞利浦电话1-800-345-6443，按2后按1。医院客户请拨打1-800-722-9377，按2。不良反应或质量问题可向MedWatch（FDA安全信息及不良事件报告系统）报告。
    [Original] Ventilator Correction: Philips Issues Correction for Trilogy Evo Platform Ventilators
    The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has classified the recall of certain Philips Respironics Trilogy ventilators as its most serious type, warning that continued use without correction may cause serious injury or death. The recall involves correcting the devices through software updates and does not require their removal from use or sale. Philips has identified three issues affecting the Trilogy Evo Platform ventilators, including the Trilogy Evo, Evo O2, EV300, and Evo Universal models. The first issue involves hazards associated with nebulizer use. When a nebulizer—pneumatic or non-pneumatic—is used inline with a passive circuit during AC-VC, SIMV, or AVAPS modes, clinicians may see discrepancies in tidal volume delivery and monitoring. Pneumatic nebulizers may affect ventilator performance across all circuit types. Volume delivery accuracy may be impacted by added gas or changes in gas properties, causing displayed patient volume and flow monitoring to not reflect actual values. Triggering sensitivity may also be affected. Aerosolized particles can impact circuit components, including external flow sensors and passive exhalation valves. Potential harms include hypoventilation, patient discomfort, and delayed clinical response. Additionally, aerosol buildup on internal flow sensors may cause over-delivery of tidal volume, under-delivery of FiO2, or a Ventilatory Inoperative condition, leading to lung overinflation, low oxygen saturation, or absence of therapy. The second issue involves delayed obstruction alarms. In certain ventilation modes, the alarm may not trigger within the required timeframe of two breath cycles or five seconds, and could be delayed by up to four breaths. As of March 6, Philips has reported three serious injuries and no deaths associated with these issues. Philips issued a letter on March 2 and a follow-up on April 24 with updated guidance. The company recommends updating all Trilogy Evo Platform ventilators to software version 1.05.15.00 and reviewing the latest User Manual Addendum. Users are advised to exercise caution when using nebulizers with affected devices. These ventilators provide CPAP and intermittent positive airway pressure support for pediatric through adult patients weighing at least 2.5 kg, used in hospitals, healthcare settings, and nonemergency transport. The EV300 and Evo may also be used in homes. U.S. DME and homecare customers with questions should contact Philips at 1-800-345-6443, option 2, then option 1. Hospital customers should call 1-800-722-9377, option 2. Adverse reactions or quality problems may be reported to MedWatch, the FDA Safety Information and Adverse Event Reporting Program.

    [Original] Pharma leaders meet with PM Takaichi in push for Japan to retain R&D edge
    More than 20 pharmaceutical leaders from Japan, the U.S., and Europe met with Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi this week to discuss efforts to bolster Japan’s position in life sciences amid China’s growing dominance in biopharma R&D. The meeting, described by Roche CEO Thomas Schinecker as “an important opportunity to discuss the actions needed for Japan to retain its leadership in life sciences,” was organized by the Biopharmaceutical CEO Roundtable (BCR), which Schinecker chairs, under the auspices of the International Federation of Pharmaceutical Manufacturers and Associations (IFPMA). “Investment into innovative medicines and vaccines is not only essential for health outcomes, but it is also a strategic investment underpinning Japan’s economic growth, national security and resilience,” Schinecker said in a May 26 release. “We must work together to address the current decline in Japan’s share of global biopharmaceutical R&D and ensure that Japanese patients do not face a widening gap in access to life-saving treatments.” The IFPMA noted that biopharma companies have invested 14 trillion Japanese yen ($87.9 billion) in R&D in Japan over the past decade, resulting in more than 1,200 new medicines. However, policy changes and increasing global competition have contributed to Japan’s “structurally declining” share of global investments, as early-stage pipelines and clinical trial activity shift elsewhere. The IFPMA specifically cited the U.S.’ Most Favored Nation drug pricing policy, which ties U.S. drug prices to those in certain high-income countries, as putting Japan at a “growing competitive disadvantage.” This external pressure, combined with “repeated revisions to drug prices rules,” could widen the gap between medicines available in Japan and other countries. To address this, the group of global pharma leaders contends that boosting Japan’s government spending on innovative medicines is “core to addressing this gap.” Daiichi Sankyo CEO and BCR vice chair Hiroyuki Okuzawa added, “Japan remains one of the traditional homes of pharmaceutical innovation, and will continue to be central to the future strength of Japan. Continued innovation will be critical in successfully tackling demographic change, emerging health challenges and evolving global risks.” Schinecker noted in a LinkedIn post that the meeting with Takaichi and her cabinet was a “constructive and valuable exchange,” adding, “Our message was clear: investing into healthcare innovation is vital to Japan’s health outcomes, economic growth and resilience.” The BCR previously addressed the “innovation gap” in Japan during a 2023 meeting with Japanese officials, proposing a “public-private strategic dialogue” on drug pricing reforms and R&D efforts. The discussions come as countries like the U.K. increase focus on global competition, exemplified by U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer and AstraZeneca CEO Pascal Soriot meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping earlier this year, resulting in a $15 billion investment pledge from AstraZeneca for drug discovery, clinical development, and manufacturing in China through 2030.
