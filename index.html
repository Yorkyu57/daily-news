    [Original] FDA Takes Further Steps to Remove Outdated Authorizations for Color Additives in Food
    The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has announced two significant regulatory actions targeting petroleum-based color additives in food, aligning with the agency’s broader initiative to “Make America Healthy Again.” The FDA is issuing a final order to revoke the authorized use of Orange B as a food color additive and is proposing to revoke the authorization for Citrus Red No. 2. Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., stated, “The Trump Administration is taking decisive action to strengthen the safety of America’s food supply.” He emphasized that by eliminating outdated authorizations for petroleum-based color additives no longer in use, the administration is modernizing food safety regulations. The proposed revocation of Citrus Red No. 2 stems from the FDA’s tentative conclusion that its use has been abandoned by the industry. Authorized since 1959, Citrus Red No. 2 was originally used for coloring the skins of mature oranges. Acting FDA Commissioner Kyle Diamantas, J.D., noted, “The FDA is committed to maintaining a science-based, modern regulatory framework that reflects current manufacturing practices and marketplace realities.” He added that removing these outdated authorizations under President Trump’s regulatory reform agenda ensures regulations remain effective, transparent, and aligned with the agency’s public health mission. Regarding Orange B, the FDA issued a final order revoking its authorization after evaluating public comments on the proposed action. The agency received no information that altered its conclusion that the industry had abandoned the use of Orange B. The FDA is currently accepting public comments on the proposed revocation of Citrus Red No. 2 for a 30-day period. All comments must be submitted by August 24, 2026. Submissions can be made electronically through the Federal eRulemaking Portal to docket FDA-2026-N-6304 or by mail to the Dockets Management Staff at 5630 Fishers Lane, Room 1061, Rockville, MD 20852. Following the review of these comments, the FDA will determine whether to finalize the revocation. These actions represent significant milestones in the Administration’s effort to phase out petroleum-based color additives from the U.S. food supply. The FDA continues to track voluntary industry commitments to remove these dyes through its “Tracking Food Industry Pledges to Remove Petroleum-Based Food Dyes” webpage, which summarizes commitments from manufacturers, retailers, and trade associations as they transition to alternative color sources. The FDA, an agency within the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, protects public health by ensuring the safety and security of the nation’s food supply, drugs, vaccines, medical devices, cosmetics, dietary supplements, and tobacco products. For media inquiries, contact the FDA Request for Comment line at 202-690-6343.
