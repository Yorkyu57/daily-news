    [Original] FDA rolls out new streamlined adverse event monitoring system, eyes $120M in savings
    The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has officially launched the FDA Adverse Event Monitoring System (AEMS), a unified platform designed to consolidate seven fragmented reporting databases into a single, intuitive interface. Unveiled on March 11, 2026, the initiative aims to modernize post-marketing surveillance and provide "radical transparency" regarding the safety of regulated products. The FDA projects the consolidation will save approximately $120 million over the next five years, addressing what Commissioner Marty Makary, M.D., Ph.D., described as "outdated and fragmented" systems that previously wasted "millions of taxpayer dollars" and created dangerous blind spots. Previously, the agency processed roughly six million adverse event reports annually across seven difficult-to-use databases, costing approximately $37 million yearly to operate. AEMS will centralize reports for drugs, biologics, vaccines, cosmetics, and animal food into a "single streamlined dashboard." While the system is currently active, it is scheduled to contain real-time adverse event reports for all FDA-regulated products by the end of May 2026. In the interim, the agency is migrating historical data and decommissioning legacy systems. The launch has already rendered the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS), and two animal drug databases defunct. Three additional systems covering medical devices, food, diet supplements, and tobacco products will be officially replaced by AEMS in May. Beyond adverse events, AEMS will centralize consumer complaints, regulatory misconduct reports, and whistleblower submissions across all FDA centers. The agency explicitly warns that AEMS data alone do not indicate a product's safety profile or establish causality between a drug and an adverse event. Furthermore, the platform cannot track specific incidence rates, and reported information remains unverified. However, the FDA notes these reports are vital for identifying "potential safety signals, such as patterns or clusters of adverse events that might indicate previously unknown risks." This modernization follows the FDA's April decision to update FAERS daily rather than quarterly. The move also addresses long-standing criticisms from Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who previously called it "outrageous that we don't have a surveillance system that functions." VAERS was previously the subject of a standoff between Kennedy and former Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research Director Peter Marks, M.D., Ph.D. Marks resigned after attempting to block Kennedy's full access to the database, fearing his team would "write over it or erase the whole database." Kennedy, a longtime vaccine skeptic, has previously cited VAERS reports to claim the COVID-19 vaccine was "the deadliest vaccine ever made."
