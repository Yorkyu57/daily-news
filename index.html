  • 亚马逊将停止接受新的机械 Turk 客户
    亚马逊宣布将停止接受机械 Turks（Mechanical Turk）的新客户，这标志着该众包服务运营生命周期的潜在终结。根据发布在机械 Turk 网站上的声明，该平台将于 2026 年 7 月 30 日关闭新用户注册。亚马逊网络服务（AWS）表示，这一决定是经过“慎重考虑”后作出的，并澄清称，现有客户可以继续无中断地使用该服务，但公司没有计划推出新功能。AWS 确认其持续投资于安全性和可用性改进，但证实该服务实际上处于“维持生命”状态，而非立即完全关闭。 机械 Turk 于 2005 年推出，最初作为一个市场运作，工人通过完成难以完全自动化的简单任务获得小额报酬，例如完成验证码挑战或识别句子情感。该服务成为关于众包劳动伦理辩论的核心，并在 Facebook-Cambridge Analytica 丑闻的早期阶段发挥了次要作用。到 2018 年，亚马逊将该平台重新定位为一家工具，供公司用于标注数据以在其 SageMaker AI 服务中训练神经网络。 机械 Turk 与人工智能之间的关系变得越来越复杂。该平台被描述为采用“先假装再实现”（fake-it-till-you-make-it）方法的人工智能公司的幕后推手，即那些被宣传为智能的产品实际上是由人类工人驱动的。这种动态与最初的机械 Turk 相呼应，后者本身就是一个骗局，其中隐藏了一位假装成机器的人类国际象棋选手。 进一步复杂化的是，2023年的一项分析显示，平台上33%至46%的工人使用大型语言模型来完成他们的任务。这一统计数据引发了关于平台标注数据可靠性的重大疑问，以及人类监督是否仍然必要。在最近的公告之后，一位Reddit用户指出，该平台实际上“多年前”就已经死亡，理由是工人和研究者因机器人和欺诈行为而放弃使用。该用户预测，亚马逊最终可能会认为维护服务器是浪费时间和资源，从而导致全面关闭。 TechCrunch周末编辑兼本报告作者Anthony Ha此前曾在Adweek担任科技记者，在VentureBeat担任高级编辑，在Hollister Free Lance担任地方政府记者，并在一家风险投资公司担任内容副总裁。他居住在纽约市，可通过anthony.ha@techcrunch.com联系。
    [Original] Amazon will stop accepting new customers for Mechanical Turk
    Amazon has announced that it will cease accepting new customers for Mechanical Turk, signaling the potential end of the crowdsourcing service’s operational life. According to a statement posted on the Mechanical Turk website, the platform will close to new users on July 30, 2026. Amazon Web Services (AWS) stated that the decision followed “careful consideration,” clarifying that while existing customers can continue to use the service without interruption, the company does not plan to introduce new features. AWS affirmed its ongoing investment in security and availability improvements but confirmed that the service is effectively on life support rather than being completely shut down immediately. Launched in 2005, Mechanical Turk originally functioned as a marketplace where workers were paid small sums to perform simple tasks resistant to full automation, such as completing CAPTCHA challenges or identifying sentence sentiment. The service became central to debates regarding the ethics of crowdsourced labor and played a minor role in the early stages of the Facebook-Cambridge Analytica scandal. By 2018, Amazon rebranded the platform as a tool for companies to annotate data to train neural networks within its SageMaker AI service. The relationship between Mechanical Turk and artificial intelligence has grown increasingly complex. The platform has been described as a hidden enabler for companies adopting a “fake-it-till-you-make-it” approach to AI, where products marketed as intelligent are actually powered by human workers. This dynamic mirrors the original Mechanical Turk, which was itself a hoax featuring a hidden human chess player pretending to be a machine. Complicating matters further, a 2023 analysis revealed that between 33% and 46% of workers on the platform were using large language models to complete their tasks. This statistic raised significant questions about the reliability of data annotated on the platform and whether human oversight remained necessary. Following the recent announcement, a Reddit user suggested the platform had effectively died “years ago,” citing abandonment by workers and researchers due to bots and fraud. The user predicted that Amazon might eventually decide that maintaining the servers is a waste of time and resources, leading to a complete shutdown.     China is leading a global race to solve the most significant challenge in robotics: creating dextrous robotic hands capable of replicating human dexterity. While humanoid robots have gained visibility, such as Unitree’s performance at the 2025 Spring Gala, they remain largely gimmicky. Experts, including Elon Musk, note that hands represent the majority of engineering difficulty in humanoid design. However, Chinese startups are leveraging the country’s manufacturing prowess and government support for “embodied AI” to transform these machines into useful products. The Chinese government has identified embodied-intelligence robots as a sector opening up new trillion-yuan markets, a critical development as China faces an aging and shrinking workforce. Despite installing more than half of the world’s factory robots annually, true multipurpose humanoids are still far off, according to the International Federation of Robotics. The bottleneck lies in the hands, which require ten times the dexterity of other body parts but occupy only one-tenth of the volume. Zhou Yong, founder of LinkerBot, emphasizes that making a robotic hand is “one hundred times more difficult” than building a humanoid. Inspired by Steve Jobs’ philosophy of focus, Zhou launched LinkerBot in 2023 to concentrate solely on hands. The company now produces approximately 5,000 hands monthly, aiming to double output while pursuing a $6bn valuation. Zhou also aims to reduce the cost of prosthetic hands from tens of thousands of dollars to just $1,000. China’s robust supply chain, bolstered by the electric vehicle industry, provides a significant hardware advantage. Pan Yunzhe, founder of Wuji Technology, returned from the US to start his company in Shenzhen because sourcing components was too constraining in America. Consequently, China has registered over 1 million robotic companies, with registrations up 40% in 2025. The dextrous hand industry alone surpassed 50bn yuan ($7.4bn) last year, a sharp increase from 13bn yuan in 2024. While hardware challenges are being addressed, software remains the harder hurdle. Nathan Lepora, a robotics professor at the University of Bristol, notes that controlling these hands is a “whole different game.” Startups are collecting vast amounts of data through teleoperation and wearable sensors, such as Wuji’s glove, which captures pressure and touch data. Pan states that capturing human movement and tactile feedback are fundamental, unsolved problems. Despite these complexities, entrepreneurs like Zhou envision a future where robotic hands build more robotic hands, ultimately serving as household helpers to enhance human prosperity rather than replace labor.
