    [Original] Dr TikTok: patients diagnose chronic illnesses with anonymous commenters’ help
    Malina Lee, a 31-year-old wedding baker from San Antonio, Texas, credits a TikTok comment from a user named "PickleFart" with saving her life by leading to her thyroid cancer diagnosis. Lee joined the platform in 2020 during pandemic lockdowns to advertise her business, never anticipating a medical breakthrough. Four years later, the anonymous commenter noted asymmetry in Lee's neck, suggesting a goiter or enlarged thyroid gland. Lee sought medical attention immediately and was diagnosed with thyroid cancer. She received prompt treatment and was declared cancer-free in less than a year. "My oncologist actually was in awe that I had caught it so early," Lee stated. "I hate to say it, but I would not have gone to the doctor unless I had seen that comment. The process was accelerated by someone called PickleFart, what can I say?" The commenter, whose real name is Billie Jean Tuomi, has become known as the "thyroid avenger." Diagnosed with thyroid cancer herself in 2012, Tuomi now scans TikTok for visible neck asymmetry to alert users to potential issues. She estimates she has correctly identified serious problems in at least four cases. "If it weren't for the original comment, I'd probably be really sick now or would still have cancer," Lee said. "I do owe my life to TikTok now." Tuomi emphasizes that early detection is vital, noting, "The earlier you get diagnosed, the easier it is to treat, so I feel like it's important to say something if you see something." This phenomenon reflects a broader trend where TikTok's algorithm and community help users identify undiagnosed conditions. Tori Mosser, a 23-year-old filmmaker, credits a viewer's suggestion of chronic appendicitis for her eventual diagnosis after being dismissed by doctors who attributed her vomiting to anxiety. "Being a woman can contribute to being dismissed in healthcare settings," Mosser explained. "I've been told I'm being dramatic... It's frustrating and it's invalidating." However, medical professionals warn of the risks. Craig Mittleman, director of emergency services at Lawrence + Memorial hospital, noted a sharp increase in patients presenting internet-influenced diagnoses. "In some ways, it's allowed patients to feel empowered," Mittleman said. "But I also find that we are often... spending a lot of time debunking information that patients present." He highlighted the danger of misinformation, stating, "Most patients and most lay folk don't know how to distinguish between good information, actionable information and misinformation." Despite these risks, Tuomi remains cautious, urging users to see doctors rather than relying on her comments. She changed her username to "PickleFart" after receiving an influx of unsolicited neck photos, remarking, "It shows how broken the American healthcare system is that people are seeking out medical advice on social media apps." For users like Lee and Mosser, the platform offers crucial community support for invisible illnesses, with Lee noting, "I really rely on my feed as a place I can go for comfort and realize I'm not alone in my experiences."
