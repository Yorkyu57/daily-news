    [Original] Breathing Circuit Recall: Medline Removes Neonatal and Infant Heated Wire Breathing Circuits
    The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has issued a recall for specific lots of Hudson RCI Neonatal/Infant Heated Wire Breathing Circuits manufactured by Medline. The agency determined that the affected devices may cause temporary or reversible health problems, or, though unlikely, serious health issues. Medline has instructed customers to immediately identify and quarantine the affected breathing circuits, removing them from use or sale, and to destroy the product once recall actions are complete. The recall stems from reports of sporadic failures involving the electrical connector of certain neonatal and infant heated wire circuits. In affected units, the heated wire connector, or pigtail, at the Neptune humidifier may exhibit discoloration, melting, smoke, a burning odor, or other signs of localized thermal damage. While the Neptune humidifier is designed with safety features that automatically discontinue power to the heated wires upon detecting electrical abnormalities, allowing respiratory gas flow to continue, the loss of heating function poses significant risks. Continued delivery of inadequately heated or unconditioned gas can desiccate and damage the airway epithelium, predisposing infants to bronchospasm, inspissated secretions, and airway obstruction. Additionally, increased circuit condensation, known as rainout, can cause inadvertent positive end-expiratory pressure, circuit obstruction, or accidental lavage of pooled fluid into the infant's airway. Reduced humidity and the delivery of cold, dry gas also increase the risk of cold stress, leading to elevated oxygen consumption and worsening respiratory distress. Medline sent letters to affected customers on July 13, July 27, and August 5, recommending the removal of all affected lots. The affected products are identified by specific labels: those manufactured before January 2024 bear a Teleflex label with item numbers such as 780-07 or 880-14KIT, while products manufactured on or after January 2024 feature a Medline label with item numbers such as HUD78007 or HUD88014KIT. As of July 31, Medline has not reported any serious injuries or deaths associated with this issue. These Hudson Heated Wire Ventilator Circuits are intended as a conduit for respiratory gas between a neonatal patient and a ventilator, utilizing heated wires to minimize condensation and maintain heating and humidification in the gas pathway. The FDA notes that the Unique Device Identifier (UDI) helps identify individual medical devices, facilitating more accurate reporting and analysis of adverse events. Health care professionals and consumers are advised to monitor ventilator, circuit, and humidification systems with increased vigilance. Customers in the U.S. with adverse reactions, quality problems, or questions should contact Medline at 866-359-1704 or recalls@medline.com. Additionally, adverse reactions or quality problems may be reported to MedWatch, the FDA Safety Information and Adverse Event Reporting Program. The FDA posts company-provided information as a public service to ensure transparency regarding device safety.

  • 介绍 @huggingface/kernels：200+ 个用于本地 AI 的 WebGPU 内核
    Hugging Face 发布了 @huggingface/kernels，这是一个极简的 JavaScript 库，旨在直接从 Hugging Face Hub 加载并执行经过优化的 WebGPU 内核。该发布由 WebAI 团队于 2026 年 9 月 1 日宣布，包含一个初始的内核集合，共 207 个内核，托管于 huggingface.co/webgpu-kernels。该计划旨在通过解决技术栈的基础层问题——即必须针对多样化的设备和浏览器实现优化单个 GPU 操作——使基于浏览器的 AI 推理尽可能快速且用户友好。 每个内核都以完整的、版本化的包形式发布，采用 Apache-2.0 许可证，包含接口定义、WGSL 着色器模板、正确性测试、基准测试用例以及使用说明。为支持这一生态系统，Hugging Face 还推出了 Fleet，一个浏览器内的 GPU 基准测试套件，允许用户在自己的硬件上运行并评估内核。在获得用户同意的情况下，Fleet 通过众包方式收集私有的性能和正确性证据，使团队能够识别特定设备的故障（如结果错误或性能极慢的情况），并在真实硬件上改进内核变体。 该库解决了 WebGPU 可移植性并不保证性能的问题；工作组大小、内存访问模式和向量化等因素会显著影响速度。通过使操作可单独发现、可测试且版本化，开发者可以在稳定的基础上构建更高级别的运行时。例如，ai.onnx.Add 内核包含针对形状相等、向量化广播和通用广播的变体，允许运行时在不改变面向应用程序的 API 的情况下选择最优实现。 在与 Apple M4 GPU 上 ORT WebGPU（ONNX Runtime Web 1.30.0-dev）的正面比较中，Hugging Face 的内核展现了卓越的性能。在 809 个有效测试用例中，新内核的几何平均速度快 2.57 倍，中位数速度快 1.90 倍，取得了 629 次胜利、176 次失败和 4 次平局。针对特定场景的优化在复杂情况下产生了显著效果；双线性 Einsum 用例的运行速度快了 10,000 倍（0.136 毫秒对比 1,396 毫秒），而按行计算的 CumSum 速度快了 301 倍。这些基准测试衡量的是 GPU 执行时间，不包括设置开销，代表的是单个操作的性能，而非完整的模型推理。 该集合作为 WebAI 生态系统的共享基础，与 Hub 上的 CUDA、ROCm 和 Metal 内核并列。Hugging Face 正与 ONNX Runtime 团队合作，将这些改进上游化，以确保 ONNX Runtime Web 生态系统获得更广泛的益处。这 207 个内核只是一个起点，计划扩展操作覆盖范围，并将这些底层组件与更高级的模型工具连接起来，以促进在整个 Web 上实现快速的本地推理。
    [Original] Introducing @huggingface/kernels: 200+ WebGPU Kernels for Local AI
    Hugging Face has released @huggingface/kernels, a minimal JavaScript library designed to load and execute optimized WebGPU kernels directly from the Hugging Face Hub. Announced on September 1, 2026, by the WebAI team, the release includes an initial collection of 207 kernels hosted at huggingface.co/webgpu-kernels. This initiative aims to make browser-based AI inference as fast and user-friendly as possible by addressing the foundational layer of the stack, where individual GPU operations must be optimized for diverse devices and browser implementations. Each kernel is published as a complete, versioned package under the Apache-2.0 license, containing interface definitions, WGSL shader templates, correctness tests, benchmark cases, and usage instructions. To support this ecosystem, Hugging Face is also launching Fleet, an in-browser GPU benchmarking suite that allows users to run and score kernels on their own hardware. With user consent, Fleet crowdsources private performance and correctness evidence, enabling the team to identify device-specific failures, such as incorrect results or pathologically slow cases, and improve kernel variants across real-world hardware. The library addresses the challenge that WebGPU portability does not guarantee performance; factors like workgroup sizes, memory access patterns, and vectorization significantly impact speed. By making operations individually discoverable, testable, and versioned, developers can build higher-level runtimes on a stable foundation. For example, the ai.onnx.Add kernel includes variants for equal shapes, vectorized broadcasting, and general broadcasting, allowing the runtime to select the optimal implementation without changing the application-facing API. In head-to-head comparisons against ORT WebGPU (ONNX Runtime Web 1.30.0-dev) on an Apple M4 GPU, Hugging Face’s kernels demonstrated superior performance. Across 809 valid test cases, the new kernels were 2.57x faster by geometric mean and 1.90x faster at the median, recording 629 wins, 176 losses, and 4 ties. Specific optimizations yielded dramatic results in complex scenarios; a bilinear Einsum case ran 10,000x faster (0.136 ms vs. 1,396 ms), and a row-wise CumSum was 301x faster. These benchmarks measure GPU execution time, excluding setup overhead, and represent individual operation performance rather than full model inference. The collection serves as a shared foundation for the WebAI ecosystem, sitting alongside CUDA, ROCm, and Metal kernels on the Hub. Hugging Face is collaborating with the ONNX Runtime team to upstream these improvements, ensuring broader benefits for the ONNX Runtime Web ecosystem. The 207 kernels represent a starting point, with plans to expand operation coverage and connect these low-level components to higher-level model tooling to facilitate fast local inference across the web.

Angel investors included Zach Frankel (president of Cognition), Yinon Costica (co-founder of Wiz), Ofir Erlich (co-founder of Eon), Anne Neuberger, Omer Adam, Varun Anand (co-founder of Clay), Swish, and Netz. Saban argues that while AI agent usage resembles operating systems, the tools they install lack the oversight historically applied to drivers. “You don’t have that with skills or plugins or MCPs, and it’s a shame, because it’s the same mechanism, it’s the same lesson, but we haven’t learned it,” Saban said. The primary risk is that attackers can poison content consumed by autonomous agents rather than attacking the agents directly. AIR’s solution involves a visibility product to identify active agents and unapproved AI tool usage, an enforcement layer to intercept and analyze agent actions, and a whitelist system that filters out approximately 27% of online add-ons and skills. AIR claims more than 20 customers, roughly a quarter of which are large enterprises, with the strongest demand in heavily regulated industries like financial services and pharmaceuticals. Bogomil Balkansky, partner at Sequoia, stated, “This is not a scanning problem, it is a continuous re-verification problem... Inspecting every skill, plugin, MCP server and sub-agent an enterprise’s agents touch, re-inspecting each one every time it changes, in real time and across an entire company’s agent fleet, is an infrastructure problem long before it is a security problem. Air has spent the last year building that pipeline. You do not catch up to it by writing a better scanner.” AIR currently employs around 40 people. Saban said the new capital will primarily fund the hiring of researchers and the expansion of go-to-market efforts in the U.S. and Europe. While acknowledging that AI labs will eventually build in security checks, Saban believes enterprises will still prefer independent products that work across multiple vendors.
