    [Original] Serif, Flagship’s latest biotech, aims to make a new kind of genetic medicine
    Flagship Pioneering has launched Serif Biomedicines, a biotechnology startup armed with $50 million in funding, to develop a new class of "modified DNA" therapies. The company claims its approach can solve persistent problems in genetic medicine by combining strengths from multiple established technologies, including gene therapy and the messenger RNA (mRNA) and small-interfering RNA (siRNA) platforms used by companies like Moderna and Alnylam Pharmaceuticals. According to CEO and co-founder Jacob Rubens, Serif's treatments consist of two key components delivered via lipid nanoparticles: finely tuned DNA instructions for a therapeutic protein and an mRNA sequence for "co-factors" that help the treatment enter a cell’s nucleus. The company asserts that its modifications to synthetic DNA enable durable and safe gene expression without altering a cell’s genome—a risk associated with some gene therapies that can cause unintended health problems. The accompanying co-factors are designed to enhance the treatments' effects. By using specially designed lipid nanoparticles for delivery instead of engineered viruses, Serif aims to overcome other major limitations. This strategy is intended to allow for treatments that can be dosed more than once and reach a wide array of tissue types. The company states these properties should result in therapies that are less complicated and cheaper to produce, with potential for "broad medical applications" and "significant market opportunities." “Instead of making proteins outside of the body and injecting those proteins into us, we can actually make any drug inside of us by just delivering the DNA sequence that codes for that drug,” Rubens said. Serif plans to unveil preclinical data at an upcoming medical meeting and is initially focused on rare genetic diseases and immunology, though specific first targets remain undisclosed. The launch comes amid a challenging funding environment for cell and gene therapy developers, but Flagship, known for backing broad platforms, continues to invest. “When a foundational layer of biology becomes engineerable, new therapeutic categories are born that can redefine the possibilities for medicine,” said Noubar Afeyan, Flagship’s CEO and a Serif co-founder. Serif is Flagship's first publicly launched company this year, following 2025 debuts of AI drug discovery startups like Lila Sciences and Expedition Medicines. The company's name draws an analogy to typography, where small strokes affect perception, similar to how DNA modifications can alter a cell's response to medicine. Rubens likened the technology to adding new software: “Just like when you get a new app on your phone, you add a new capability. That’s what we do when we deliver DNA to the cell.”
