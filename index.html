    [Original] AI videos of sexualised black women removed from TikTok after BBC investigation
    TikTok has banned 20 accounts following a BBC investigation revealing the use of AI-generated Black female influencers to drive traffic to sites promoting sexually explicit content. This issue represents a growing trend on Instagram and TikTok, criticized by researchers as racist, exploitative, and misleading due to the use of racial tropes and language. The BBC, collaborating with analysts Jeremy Carrasco and Angel Nulani from the independent AI publication Riddance, identified 60 accounts, primarily on Instagram, featuring highly sexualized digital characters. These accounts often displayed exaggerated body shapes, skimpy swimwear, and artificially darkened skin tones. Account names frequently included terms like "black," "noir," "dark," and "ebony," while posts contained comments such as "loves white men" and "why I need a white guy in my life." Although the third-party sites linked from these accounts labeled the imagery as AI-generated, the social media accounts themselves failed to disclose this, breaching platform guidelines. Nearly all identified accounts were on Instagram, with about a third also present on TikTok. While many accounts did not link to paid content, the 60 identified carried chains of links to paid sexually explicit material. One specific account, which amassed three million followers within weeks of its December creation, sparked further outrage by stealing videos from real Malaysian model Riya Ulan. The account overlaid an AI-generated face with an exceptionally dark, artificial skin tone onto Ulan's body, replicating her movements and clothing. "I was angry," Ulan told the BBC. "Of course my videos are all out there… It doesn't mean that you can just take it and steal it and post it as your own." One manipulated video reached over 35 million views on TikTok and 173 million on Instagram, approximately 47 times the views of Ulan's original post. While the stolen videos were not sexual, the account used the same character for revealing content leading to paid adult sites. Ulan noted, "I'm not sure if I'm more concerned about them taking my video to promote their explicit content or [that] people actually believe in that." Researchers condemned the trend. Nulani stated, "I believe these accounts are racist because their existence perpetuates a long history of the exploitation of black people," adding that they prioritize capitalizing on Black women for the "online porn machine." Carrasco noted that AI allows for "shameless, racist depictions" and creates dark skin tones that are not natural without social consequences. Moroccan model Houda Fonone described the trend as "erasure," arguing it reinforces stereotypes by suggesting Black beauty is only acceptable when "refined." Following the BBC's inquiry, a TikTok spokesperson confirmed the removal of content and banning of accounts that breach rules, stating, "TikTok prohibits AI generated content of individuals used without their permission, we have zero tolerance for content which promotes off-platform sexual services." Meta told the BBC it was investigating the content, noting it has policies for labeling AI-generated content. Nine tracked Instagram accounts have since disappeared, though neither platform provided details regarding their response to Ulan's initial reports.
