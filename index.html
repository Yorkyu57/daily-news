    [Original] FDA mandates label updates to common Parkinson's meds based on seizure risk finding
    On March 23, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) mandated a new safety warning for common Parkinson's disease drug combinations containing levodopa and carbidopa. This regulatory action follows the identification of 14 cases of seizures linked to vitamin B6 deficiency in patients using these medications. The agency requires manufacturers to update prescribing information to highlight the risk of vitamin B6 deficiency and associated seizures. Healthcare providers are advised to evaluate patients' baseline vitamin B6 levels before and during treatment, supplementing the vitamin as necessary. The label update impacts a wide range of products, including Amneal's Crexont and Rytary, Avion Pharmaceuticals' Dhivy, Merck's Sinemet and Sinemet CR, Novartis' Stalevo, and AbbVie's Duopa. Also affected is AbbVie's Vyalev, a subcutaneous treatment approved in 2024 that delivers foscarbidopa and foslevodopa via an infusion pump. While levodopa has been a standard treatment since the 1960s for addressing dopamine deficiency, the FDA noted that its conversion to dopamine impacts vitamin B6 levels. Furthermore, carbidopa binds to the active form of the vitamin, further depleting levels. The FDA's safety review identified 14 seizure cases: 13 submitted to the adverse events reporting system and one from medical literature. The agency acknowledged there are "likely additional cases about which we were unaware." All reviewed cases involved daily levodopa doses exceeding 1,000 mg across oral formulations and enteral suspensions. Some cases progressed to status epilepticus, a severe condition potentially causing brain damage or death. Clinical evidence supported vitamin B6 deficiency in several reports, with nine seizures resolving after vitamin B6 supplementation. Tragically, two deaths occurred in patients with "documented low vitamin B6 levels and poorly controlled seizures." "Based on the available data, FDA concluded there is reasonable evidence of a causal association between drug products containing carbidopa/levodopa and vitamin B6 deficiency-associated seizures," the agency stated. Although no seizures were confirmed for Stalevo or Vyalev, the FDA applied the same warning based on "biological plausibility" and clinical trial data. This safety sweep may bolster interest in Supernus Pharmaceuticals' Onapgo, a wearable pump delivering apomorphine. Approved in 2025 after three rejections, Onapgo paused new patient deliveries in November due to "stronger than expected demand." Shipments have since resumed. In 2025, Onapgo generated $17.3 million in sales, while AbbVie's Vyalev earned $482 million and its older Duopa garnered $381 million.
