    [Original] Father of man who inspired Super Mario was also named Luigi, researcher finds
    Gaming enthusiasts have long known that Nintendo named its iconic plumber, Super Mario, after Mario Segale, the company's landlord in Tukwila, Washington. However, a recent discovery by Elisabeth Zetland, a senior researcher at genealogy service MyHeritage, reveals that Nintendo may have unknowingly named Mario's fictional brother, Luigi, after Segale's real-life father. Zetland made this finding while exploring Segale's ancestry, prompted by the release of *The Super Mario Galaxy Movie*. She told the Guardian that while the connection appears to be "just a coincidence," it could be one of the most significant in video game history. Super Mario creator Shigeru Miyamoto confirmed in 2015 that the character was based on Segale, whose appearance and name inspired the franchise that has since sold hundreds of millions of copies. While Miyamoto stated that "Luigi" was chosen because the Italian name rhymes with the Japanese word for "similar," other theories suggested a local pizza parlor named "Mario & Luigi's" as the source. Nintendo did not immediately respond to inquiries regarding whether they knew Segale's father shared the name. Zetland's research uncovered that Luigi Maria Segale was born in 1886 in Favale di Malvaro, Italy, to a bricklayer. In 1909, he and his brother Giuseppe sailed to Ellis Island aboard the *Prinzess Irene*, eventually settling in Tukwila. Adopting the anglicized name Louis, he served in the US armed forces during World War I before becoming an independent farmer. His journey mirrored that of four million Italians who immigrated to the US between 1880 and 1915. By 1940, Louis and his wife Rina had achieved financial stability, growing produce like tomatoes and onions to sell in Seattle markets. Records show the family celebrated Mario's 12th birthday in 1946 with a Ligurian-style meal and an accordion performance in their newly purchased Seattle home. The couple later received recognition from the *Catholic Northwest Progress* for supporting the archdiocese's foster program. "It's really a legacy of Italian dreams and American opportunity," Zetland noted. Mario Segale built a successful construction and real estate career before his death in 2018. Nintendo, one of his tenants, named their character after him following an incident where Segale confronted staff about overdue rent, as detailed in David Sheff's book *Game Over*. Segale remained press-shy about the connection, telling the *Seattle Times* in 1993, "You might say I'm still waiting for my royalty checks." His obituary noted he "always ducked the notoriety." Louis Segale died in 1981, five years before *Super Mario Bros* introduced Luigi to the world. In the 2023 animated film, the fictional father, voiced by Charles Martinet, remains unnamed in the credits.

Her visit, the first by a Mexican president in eight years, coincided with a meeting with Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, the summit's co-chair. Diplomatic friction had peaked in 2019 when Sheinbaum's predecessor, Andrés Manuel López Obrador, demanded a Spanish apology for human rights violations during the 16th-century dismantling of the Aztec empire. After Spain declined to apologize, Sheinbaum excluded King Felipe VI from her 2024 inauguration, prompting Spain to reciprocate by sending no representative. However, recent developments indicate a shift. In March, King Felipe acknowledged "a lot of abuse" during the conquest, stating that such historical events "cannot make us feel proud" by modern standards. This marked the first public admission of colonial-era abuses by a Spanish monarch. Subsequently, Sheinbaum invited the King to the upcoming FIFA World Cup, describing it as a "timely opportunity to evoke the depth and unique character of the ties between Mexico and Spain." Additionally, Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares previously acknowledged "pain and injustice" in the shared history, earning praise from Sheinbaum. The Barcelona gathering, the fourth meeting of the "In Defence of Democracy" initiative, aimed to counter rising illiberalism and extremism. Sánchez warned that "democracy cannot be taken for granted," citing attacks on the multilateral system and the "dangerous normalisation of the use of force."     [Original] French film star Nathalie Baye dies aged 77, media report
    Nathalie Baye, one of France's most celebrated film stars, has died at the age of 77, French media report. The much-loved stalwart of French cinema passed away at her home in Paris on Friday evening from a form of dementia, specifically Lewy body dementia, according to her family. President Emmanuel Macron honored the actress, stating she was "an actress with whom we loved, dreamed and grew up." Macron further noted on X that with her voice, smile, and grace, she remained a constant presence in French cinema over the past few decades, spanning works from François Truffaut to Tonie Marshall. Born in Normandy in 1948 into a bohemian family of artists, Baye began her career as a dancer before transitioning to acting. She secured her breakthrough role in François Truffaut's 1973 comedy *La Nuit Américaine* (*Day for Night*) shortly after graduating from drama school. Over a career spanning five decades, she starred in approximately 80 films and became one of France's most decorated actresses. A four-time winner at the Césars, France's equivalent of the Oscars, Baye won her first award in 1981 for her supporting role in Jean-Luc Godard's *Sauve qui peut (la vie)* (*Every Man for Himself*). She was named best supporting actress again in 1982 and won best actress the following year for *Une Étrange Affaire* (*Strange Affair*) and *La Balance*. Additionally, she received the best actress award at the 1999 Venice Film Festival for *Une Liaison Pornographique* (*An Affair of Love*). Baye's international profile grew with roles in high-profile projects, including Steven Spielberg's 2002 film *Catch Me if You Can*, where she played Leonardo DiCaprio's mother. More recently, she appeared in the acclaimed series *Call My Agent!* alongside her daughter, actress Laura Smet, and played a French aristocrat in the second *Downton Abbey* film. Her final on-screen role was in the 2023 Franco-Lebanese drama *La nuit du verre d'eau* (*Mother Valley*). Away from acting, Baye publicly supported action to tackle climate change and reform French assisted dying legislation. She had a five-year relationship with rock artist Johnny Hallyday, nicknamed the French Elvis, with whom she had Smet. Tributes poured in from colleagues, including Isabelle Adjani, who called her an "actress of dazzling spontaneity," and Richard Berry, who wrote, "Nathalie, you take with you our laughter and the fond memories of our early days." Culture Minister Catherine Pégard told AFP that Baye had "lit up a long chapter in the history of French cinema with her talent and radiant personality."
