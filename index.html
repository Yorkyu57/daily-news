    美国食品药品监督管理局（FDA）宣布，德康公司（Dexcom, Inc.）被选为数字健康设备“技术赋能有意义患者结局”（TEMPO）试点计划的首家制造商。该举措旨在支持医疗保险和医疗补助服务中心（CMS）创新中心的ACCESS（以有效、可扩展的解决方案推进慢性病护理）模型，致力于改善美国慢性病患者健康结局。 FDA设备与放射健康中心主任Michelle Tarver医学博士、哲学博士表示：“对于数百万患有慢性病的美国人而言，在正确的时间获得合适的技术可能改变人生。”她指出，选定首位TEMPO参与者标志着与CMS创新中心合作试点一种方法的重要里程碑，该方法旨在贴近患者实际需求，并生成真实世界证据，以确保技术能够带来切实成效。 被选中的设备是德康血糖健康计划（Dexcom Glucose Health Program），该计划针对ACCESS模型中的两个临床应用领域。它使患者和医疗专业人员能够监测代谢和营养状况，获得个性化指导，并访问实时数据和人工智能洞察，以支持知情决策和行为改变。该计划通过整合数字健康指标，有助于筛查糖尿病前期和2型糖尿病，并可能帮助糖尿病前期人群改善血糖控制并降低糖化血红蛋白（HbA1c）水平。 CMS副行政长官兼创新中心总监Abe Sutton表示：“首位TEMPO参与者代表了在现代化创新技术触达医疗保险受益人方面迈出的重要一步。”他解释说，ACCESS模型提供了一条奖励可衡量健康改善的支付路径，而TEMPO则生成负责任地评估新技术所需的真实世界证据。 参与制造商将收集、监测并报告其设备预期用途的真实世界数据（RWD），无论这些设备是由制造商直接提供还是由其他ACCESS项目参与者提供。这有助于美国食品药品监督管理局（FDA）和联邦医疗保险与医疗补助服务中心（CMS）了解数字健康技术在真实世界环境中的表现，以支持慢性病护理。 FDA计划在ACCESS模型临床用途领域的四个领域中，各选择最多十家TEMPO试点参与者，以反映美国本土制造商的广泛代表性。选择标准包括对患者健康的潜在风险、对患者获益的合理预期、真实世界数据收集计划以及与ACCESS模型结果的一致性。TEMPO试点与数字健康设备的快速发展相一致，旨在扩大患者可及性的同时保障安全性。ACCESS模型的第一批参与者于2026年7月启动，针对心脏-肾脏-代谢、肌肉骨骼和行为健康疾病。FDA继续接受试点项目的意向声明，且未设定具体截止日期。
    [Original] FDA Announces First Participant Selected for TEMPO for Digital Health Devices Pilot
    The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has announced the selection of Dexcom, Inc. as the first manufacturer for the Technology-Enabled Meaningful Patient Outcomes (TEMPO) for Digital Health Devices Pilot. This initiative is designed to support the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) Innovation Center’s ACCESS (Advancing Chronic Care with Effective, Scalable Solutions) Model, aiming to improve health outcomes for Americans managing chronic diseases. Michelle Tarver, M.D., Ph.D., Director of the FDA’s Center for Devices and Radiological Health, stated, “For the millions of Americans living with chronic conditions, access to the right technology at the right time can be life-changing.” She noted that selecting the first TEMPO participant marks a milestone in piloting an approach, in partnership with the CMS Innovation Center, to meet patients where they are and generate real-world evidence to ensure technologies deliver results. The selected device, the Dexcom Glucose Health Program, addresses two clinical use areas within the ACCESS Model. It enables patients and healthcare professionals to monitor metabolic and nutritional status, receive tailored guidance, and access real-time data and AI insights for informed decision-making and behavioral modifications. The program aids in screening for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes through integrated digital health metrics and may help improve glycemic control and lower HbA1c in people with prediabetes. Abe Sutton, CMS Deputy Administrator and Director of the Innovation Center, said, “The first TEMPO participant represents an important step toward modernizing how innovative technologies reach people with Medicare.” He explained that ACCESS provides a payment pathway rewarding measurable health improvements, while TEMPO generates the real-world evidence needed to evaluate new technologies responsibly. Participating manufacturers will collect, monitor, and report real-world data (RWD) regarding their devices’ intended uses, whether offered directly or by other ACCESS participants. This helps the FDA and CMS understand how digital health technologies perform in real-world settings to support chronic disease care. The FDA plans to select up to ten TEMPO pilot participants in each of four ACCESS model clinical use areas, reflecting a broad spectrum of U.S.-based manufacturers. Selection criteria include potential risks to patient health, reasonable expectations of patient benefit, RWD collection plans, and alignment with ACCESS model outcomes. The TEMPO pilot aligns with the rapid development of digital health devices to expand patient access while safeguarding safety. The ACCESS model’s first cohort began in July 2026, targeting cardio-kidney-metabolic, musculoskeletal, and behavioral health conditions. The FDA continues to accept statements of interest for the pilot with no specified deadline.
